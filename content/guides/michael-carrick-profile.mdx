**Michael Carrick is a former England and Manchester United midfielder who won five Premier League titles and the Champions League as a player, and who has been Manchester United's head coach since January 2026.** Born in Wallsend on 28 July 1981, he made 464 appearances for United between 2006 and 2018, coached Middlesbrough from 2022 to 2025, and in May 2026 was given the United job on a contract to 2028 after taking the club from seventh to third.

Six months on, the picture is very different. United were 18th in the Premier League after ten games of 2026-27. This profile covers both halves of the career: the midfielder who won five league titles under Sir Alex Ferguson, and the coach whose side are now in the relegation zone.

## The player

### West Ham and Tottenham

Carrick grew up in Wallsend, on Tyneside, played for Wallsend Boys Club, and joined West Ham's academy as a teenager. He won the FA Youth Cup there in 1998-99 and broke into the senior side in 1999, also spending short loans at Swindon Town and Birmingham City that season.

He was still at West Ham when they were relegated in 2002-03, and stayed for one season in the second tier, where he was named in the PFA First Division Team of the Year for 2003-04. Tottenham signed him in 2004, and he won his first full England start in May 2005, a 2-1 win over the United States.

### Manchester United, 2006-2018

United paid Tottenham £14m in August 2006, with the rest of a package worth up to £18.6m depending on appearances, honours and international caps. The fee raised eyebrows at the time. Chief executive David Gill said "we think we've got a great player at a great price", and Ferguson admitted [in the Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/sport/united-defend-carrick-fee-1.1033565) that "there's always a premium when Manchester United are buying a player", adding: "the most important thing is he retains possession."

That was the job for the next 12 years. Carrick was not a ball-winner or a goalscorer; he scored 24 goals in 464 games for the club. He was the deep midfielder who kept the ball moving, and his best seasons line up with United's last run of titles under Ferguson.

He won the Premier League in each of his first three seasons, 2006-07, 2007-08 and 2008-09, and again in 2010-11 and 2012-13. In the 2008 Champions League final in Moscow, which ended 1-1 against Chelsea, he was one of United's six scorers in the 6-5 shootout win ([Al Jazeera report](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2008/5/24/man-united-win-champions-league)). His best individual recognition came in 2012-13, Ferguson's last season: he was United's Players' Player of the Year and in the PFA Premier League Team of the Year.

The trophies kept coming after Ferguson, if less often: the FA Cup in 2016, then the League Cup and the Europa League in 2016-17. His final season was limited to a handful of games by a heart problem he said "started in the Burton game and then happened again in training". He announced his retirement in March 2018 ([Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/soccer/2018/03/12/man-united-veteran-michael-carrick-confirms-retirement-football-summer)), saying "there comes a time when as much as you don't like, it's time to stop playing football".

His last game was a fitting one. Against Watford at Old Trafford on 13 May 2018 he played a 40-yard pass to Juan Mata, who set up Marcus Rashford for the only goal, [according to the Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/sport/soccer/english-soccer/michael-carrick-bids-farewell-as-man-united-see-off-watford-1.3493760). It was his 464th appearance for the club.

### England: 34 caps over 14 years

Carrick won 34 caps between 2001 and 2015 and never scored. His debut was as a substitute in a 4-0 friendly win over Mexico in 2001; his only World Cup appearance was a 1-0 win over Ecuador in 2006; and he was an unused member of the 2010 squad in South Africa.

That tournament stayed with him. In 2018 he told BBC Two's Premier League Show, [reported by the Irish Examiner](https://www.irishexaminer.com/sport/soccer/arid-30838215.html), that he had found England duty "almost depressing in a way", and that after South Africa: "I was depressed at times, yes. I told the FA, 'Look, please don't pick me'." He was still recalled, and his last cap came against Spain in November 2015.

## The coach

### United's staff and the 2021 caretaker spell

Carrick moved straight onto José Mourinho's coaching staff in 2018, stayed when Mourinho was sacked that December, and kept the role when Ole Gunnar Solskjær was made permanent manager in March 2019. When Solskjær was sacked in November 2021, Carrick took temporary charge for three games: wins over Villarreal and Arsenal and a draw at Chelsea, [Sky Sports recorded](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11095/13493290/michael-carrick-man-utd-confirm-head-coach-appointment-for-remainder-of-the-season). He left the club after the Arsenal game.

### Middlesbrough, 2022-2025

Middlesbrough appointed him in October 2022 with the side 21st in the Championship, one point above the relegation zone. He took them to fourth, where they lost the play-off semi-final to Coventry City.

The next two seasons did not build on that. In 2023-24 Boro reached the League Cup semi-finals and beat Chelsea 1-0 in the first leg at the Riverside, before losing the second leg 6-1 at Stamford Bridge on 23 January 2024 ([Sky Sports](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11938/13054087/chelsea-6-1-middlesbrough-6-2-aggregate-cole-palmer-double-helps-hosts-reach-carabao-cup-final)). They finished eighth, four points outside the play-offs. In 2024-25 they were in the hunt until the final day, lost 2-0 at Coventry and finished 10th. The club sacked him on 4 June 2025, and his assistants Jonathan Woodgate and his brother Graeme Carrick left with him ([beIN Sports](https://www.beinsports.com/en-nz/football/championship/articles-video/middlesbrough-sacks-carrick-after-missing-play-offs-2025-06-04)).

His Boro side mainly played a 4-2-3-1, using a 4-4-2 six times and a back three five times, and led the league for high turnovers and fast breaks, according to Sky Sports.

### Manchester United head coach, 2026-

United sacked Ruben Amorim on 5 January 2026. Eight days later, after talking to Carrick and Solskjær, the club appointed Carrick head coach until the end of the season, with Steve Holland as his No.2 and Woodgate, Jonny Evans and Travis Binnion on the staff. "Having the responsibility to lead Manchester United is an honour," he said.

United were seventh, three points off the top four, with 17 league games left ([Sporting Life](https://www.sportinglife.com/football/news/manchester-united-appoint-michael-carrick-as-head-coach-until-the-end-of-the-season/229767)). His first game was a 2-0 win over Manchester City on 17 January, and United also beat Arsenal, Liverpool and Chelsea before the season was out. They won 12, drew three and lost two of his 17 games and finished third on 71 points ([Sports Mole](https://www.sportsmole.co.uk/football/man-utd/feature/man-united-2025-26-season-summary-carrick-here-to-stay-as-red-devils-secure-third-spot-in-premier-league_597390.html)), back in the Champions League a year after a 15th-place finish.

On 22 May 2026 United made the job his until 2028, with an option for a further season, [ESPN reported](https://www.espn.com/soccer/story/_/id/48789147/man-united-michael-carrick-appointed-permanent-head-coach). Director of football Jason Wilcox said he had "thoroughly earned the opportunity", and Carrick said the role "fills me with immense pride".

### 2026-27: the slump

The second season has gone badly wrong. United won one league game in August and none of the next eight, a club record in the Premier League era, and the 4-4 draw with Aston Villa at Old Trafford on 8 November, rescued by Joshua Zirkzee and a 97th-minute Patrick Dorgu header, left them 18th on eight points from ten games ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/soccer/story/_/id/50421297/man-united-aston-villa-premier-league)). Sky Sports noted it was United's lowest position after ten top-flight games since 1986-87. There was a 3-2 comeback win over Roma in the Champions League in the same week.

Asked after the Villa game whether he would walk away, Carrick told [Sky Sports](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/13609927/michael-carrick-manchester-united-boss-expects-to-stay-on-despite-club-sitting-in-relegation-zone-after-aston-villa-draw): "It's not going to happen, no. We understand the situation; we haven't won enough games."
