**The best left-back of all time is Paolo Maldini**, who played 902 games for AC Milan, won five European Cups and finished third in the Ballon d'Or twice, with Roberto Carlos and Giacinto Facchetti next. Below is the full top ten, with the career numbers and the trophies behind each pick.

## How the list is ranked

Four things, in this order:

1. **Major honours.** League titles, European Cups and Champions Leagues, World Cups and continental championships, with more weight on the ones a player was central to.
2. **Longevity at the top level.** Seasons played at a major club and caps won for a leading national team.
3. **Individual recognition.** Ballon d'Or placings, World Cup All-Star teams, the FIFA FIFPro World XI and similar selections.
4. **What they did to the position.** Some of these players changed what a left-back was expected to do.

A player only counts for the years he spent at left-back. Where a career moved on to centre-back or midfield, that is noted. Caps and appearance totals come from the club, federation or league records linked in each entry; where sources disagree, the lower or the official figure is used.

## 1. Paolo Maldini (Italy, AC Milan)

**Career:** 902 Milan games, 647 in Serie A; 126 Italy caps, 7 goals. **Honours:** 5 European Cups, 7 Serie A titles.

Maldini played his whole career for Milan, from 1984 until he [retired in 2009 aged 41](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/024e-0e99786bb0fa-6f7268847500-1000--champions-league-100-club-paolo-maldini). UEFA describes him as just as elegant at left-back as he was later at centre-back, and his five European Cups span three decades. He scored [after 52 seconds](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/0253-0d7b89fe7b7f-c91716d11bdf-1000--2004-05-liverpool-belief-defies-milan) of the 2005 final against Liverpool, the fastest goal in a European Cup final, although Milan went on to lose that one.

He finished third in the Ballon d'Or in 1994 and again in 2003, and [the FIGC](https://www.figc.it/en/fans/news/after-the-disappointment-of-the-1986-world-cup-it-was-time-for-change-maldini-was-among-those-new-faces-chosen-nvjzmh8g) lists his 126 caps as the third-highest total in Italy's history. His one gap is a tournament win with Italy: he reached the 1994 World Cup final and the Euro 2000 final and lost both. Andrea Pirlo called him "a defender who could read the game like nobody else, so intelligent and always so calm on the ball."

## 2. Roberto Carlos (Brazil, Real Madrid)

**Career:** 584 Real Madrid games, 71 goals; 125 Brazil caps, 11 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 2002, 3 Champions Leagues, 4 La Liga titles, Copa América 1997 and 1999.

No left-back has attacked like him. Roberto Carlos spent 11 seasons at Real Madrid, won the Champions League three times and was in the World Cup All-Star team in 1998 and 2002. In 2002 he finished second in the Ballon d'Or to his Brazil teammate Ronaldo.

The moment everyone remembers came on 3 June 1997 in Lyon: a free kick [from 40 yards out](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11095/10899358/roberto-carlos-brilliant-brazil-free-kick-against-france-remembered) in the 21st minute against France. "It was the kind of goal you only score once in your career," he said.

## 3. Giacinto Facchetti (Italy, Inter)

**Career:** 634 Inter games, 75 goals, 476 and 59 of them in Serie A; 94 Italy caps, 3 goals. **Honours:** Euro 1968, 2 European Cups, 4 Serie A titles.

Facchetti was one of the first great attacking full-backs, and he played for Inter from 1960 to 1978 and nowhere else. His [club record](https://www.inter.it/en/archivio_giocatore/G0229/giacinto-facchetti) of 75 goals would be good for a midfielder; in 1965-66 he scored 10 in Serie A, a record for a defender at the time. He won the European Cup in 1964 and 1965 and finished second to Eusébio in the 1965 Ballon d'Or.

For Italy he [captained the side 70 times](https://figc.it/en/fans/news/facchetti-a-player-before-his-time-in-1963-giacinto-facchetti-made-his-azzurri-debut), won the 1968 European Championship and reached the 1970 World Cup final. Inter retired his No.3 shirt after he died in 2006.

## 4. Paul Breitner (West Germany, Bayern Munich)

**Career:** 48 West Germany caps, 10 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 1974, Euro 1972, European Cup 1974, 5 Bundesliga titles, 2 La Liga titles.

Breitner ranks here for what he did at left-back in the early 1970s, when the [DFB](https://www.dfb.de/news/weltmeister-und-rebell-paul-breitner-wird-75) describes him as West Germany's left-back. In the 1974 World Cup final in Munich he equalised from the penalty spot in the 25th minute against the Netherlands, and West Germany won 2-1. "I wasn't supposed to take the spot-kick but I was nearest to the ball," he told [bundesliga.com](https://www.bundesliga.com/en/bundesliga/news/paul-breitner-revolutionary-bayern-munich-germany-24538).

He later moved into midfield, first at Real Madrid and then back at Bayern, and scored again in the 1982 final, a 3-1 defeat by Italy. He remains the only German to score in two World Cup finals, and he finished second in the 1981 Ballon d'Or.

## 5. Nílton Santos (Brazil, Botafogo)

**Career:** one club, Botafogo, 1948-64; 75 Brazil caps, 3 goals. **Honours:** World Cups 1958 and 1962.

Nicknamed "The Encyclopedia", Nílton Santos spent his whole career at Botafogo and was a pioneer of the attacking full-back. At the 1958 World Cup in Sweden he [scored in a 3-0 win over Austria](https://www.foxnews.com/sports/nilton-santos-twice-a-world-cup-winner-for-brazil-and-a-pioneer-on-defense-dies-at-88), and he was named in the tournament's All-Star team. He won the World Cup again in 1962.

His 75 caps are the [RSSSF](https://www.rsssf.org/miscellaneous/braz-recintlp.html) count, the most reliable available; some Brazilian sources give more. He was named in the FIFA 100 in 2004 and died in 2013, aged 88.

## 6. Marcelo (Brazil, Real Madrid)

**Career:** more than 540 Real Madrid games, 38 goals; 58 Brazil caps, 6 goals. **Honours:** 5 Champions Leagues, 6 La Liga titles.

Marcelo left Real Madrid in 2022 with [25 trophies in 15 years](https://www.tntsports.co.uk/football/liga/2021-2022/emotional-marcelo-says-goodbye-to-real-madrid-after-15-years-at-the-la-liga-club-and-current-champio_sto8991097/story.shtml), more than anyone in the club's history, having passed Paco Gento's record that spring. His five Champions Leagues (2014, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2022) equal Maldini's total, and he was picked in the FIFA FIFPro World XI six times, in 2012 and every year from 2015 to 2019.

He sits below the five above because his international record is thinner: no World Cup and no Copa América.

## 7. Andreas Brehme (West Germany, Inter)

**Career:** 86 West Germany and Germany caps, 8 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 1990, UEFA Cup 1991, Serie A 1989, Bundesliga 1987 and 1998.

Brehme was a left-back throughout his career and was famously two-footed. He scored the only goal of the 1990 World Cup final, an [85th-minute penalty against Argentina](https://uefa.com/news-media/news/028a-1a3a1689380a-4d899f4e33ac-1000--football-mourns-celebrated-germany-defender-andreas-brehme), and finished third in that year's Ballon d'Or. At Inter he won Serie A in 1988-89 and the UEFA Cup in 1990-91.

He died in February 2024, aged 63. "Andi was our World Cup hero, but also so much more," said Rudi Völler.

## 8. Ashley Cole (England, Arsenal and Chelsea)

**Career:** 385 Premier League games, 15 goals, 147 clean sheets; 107 England caps. **Honours:** Champions League 2012, Europa League 2013, 3 Premier League titles, 7 FA Cups.

Cole is the best left-back the Premier League has had, and he is now in its [Hall of Fame](https://www.premierleague.com/news/3936970). He won the league twice with Arsenal, including the [Invincibles season](/guides/arsenal-invincibles-unbeaten-season) of 2003-04, and once with Chelsea, and his seven FA Cups are [a record](https://dcfc.co.uk/news/2019/01/ashley-cole-career-in-numbers). In the 2012 Champions League final he scored in the shoot-out as Chelsea won the trophy for the first time.

For England he won [107 caps](https://www.englandfootball.com/england/mens-senior-team/squad/Legends-profiles/Ashley-cole) and made 22 appearances at major tournaments, an England record.

## 9. Patrice Evra (France, Manchester United)

**Career:** 273 Premier League games for Manchester United, 98 clean sheets; 81 France caps. **Honours:** Champions League 2008, 5 Premier League titles, 2 Serie A titles.

Evra won the league in five of his eight seasons at Old Trafford, and [his 273 Premier League appearances](https://www.premierleague.com/news/2510243) are the most by any non-British or Irish outfield player in United's history. He started 175 of United's 190 league games across his last five seasons, played the whole of the 2008 Champions League final win over Chelsea, and was named in the FIFA FIFPro World XI for 2009. He then won two Serie A titles with Juventus.

## 10. Jordi Alba (Spain, Barcelona)

**Career:** 458 Barcelona games, 26 goals, 91 assists; 93 Spain caps, 9 goals. **Honours:** Euro 2012, Champions League 2015, 6 La Liga titles.

Alba [left Barcelona in 2023](https://www.fcbarcelona.es/es/noticias/3486018/jordi-alba-dejara-el-barca-a-final-de-temporada/featured) with 18 trophies, and 91 assists is an exceptional return for a defender. His best-known goal came in the [Euro 2012 final](https://www.uefa.com/uefaeuro/history/news/0252-0cdc4f07099d-36a02f333f87-1000--spain-overpower-italy-to-win-uefa-euro-2012/) on 1 July 2012, when he scored Spain's second goal shortly before half-time in Spain's 4-0 win over Italy. It was his first goal for his country. "We weren't really playing with any strikers so I just ran forward without thinking about it," he told [UEFA](https://www.uefa.com/uefaeuro/history/news/0253-0d8140a9c476-dc3346b8d148-1000--euro-cult-heroes-jordi-alba-2012/).

## Close calls

**Denis Irwin (Republic of Ireland, Manchester United).** Seven Premier League titles, 529 United games and the 1999 Champions League. He missed the FA Cup final of the [1999 treble](/guides/manchester-united-1999-treble) through suspension after a red card against Liverpool. He won 56 caps for Ireland.

**Júnior (Brazil, Flamengo).** A member of Brazil's 1982 World Cup side, in which he scored in a 3-1 win over Argentina, and a winner of the Copa Libertadores and the Intercontinental Cup with Flamengo in 1981. He won 74 caps and was named in the FIFA 100.

**Ruud Krol and Antonio Cabrini.** Both appear in the top ten of [Sports Illustrated's all-time ranking](https://www.si.com/soccer/the-best-left-backs-soccer-history-ranked), which also puts Maldini first. They were left out here on the criteria above, not on reputation.

## How the left-back has changed

The order of this list also traces how the position changed. Nílton Santos and Facchetti showed in the 1950s and 1960s that a full-back could attack. Breitner and Brehme did it in World Cup finals. Roberto Carlos and Marcelo spent 26 seasons between them attacking down Real Madrid's left, and Alba's 91 assists at Barcelona show how much of a team's creativity the position can now carry. Maldini is first because he did all of it while defending as well as anyone in history.
