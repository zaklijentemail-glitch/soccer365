**There is no promotion or relegation between Liga MX and the second division at the moment: the federation's 2026-27 regulations say no Liga MX club will go down and no Liga de Expansión club will come up.** The system was suspended in April 2020, and the last club relegated on sporting grounds was Chiapas, in 2017.

That decision, published in July 2026, overturned what most of Mexican football had expected. The Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) had said in September 2025 that movement between the divisions was due back for 2026-27. It is now being fought over in a fresh CAS appeal and in the Senate.

## How the system used to work

Mexico has had a second division since 1950-51. The rule, as the statistics archive [RSSSF](https://rsssf.org/tablesm/mexall.html) records it, was simple: the last team in the first division went down and the second-division champion came up. In that first season San Sebastián de León were relegated and Zacatepec were promoted.

### The relegation table

From 1991-92 the bottom club was no longer decided by one season. Mexico introduced a relegation table, the *tabla de cocientes* or *tabla porcentual*, and Cobras de Ciudad Juárez were the first club relegated under it, according to Wikipedia's record of [that season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_Mexican_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_season).

The table divides a club's points over its last three seasons by the games it played in them. Because Liga MX splits each season into two short tournaments, the Apertura from July to December and the Clausura from January to May, that means the last six tournaments. A newly promoted club is judged only on its games since promotion. The lowest figure went down.

The point was to protect clubs from one bad year. A side with a strong record could survive a poor season; a promoted club that started badly could not.

### Certification

Before the 2017-18 season the league added a condition at the other end. A club coming up had to be certified, meeting standards for its stadium and having youth teams. If the second-division winner was not certified, the bottom Liga MX club stayed up and paid instead.

That rule is why the last few seasons before the suspension look odd. [RSSSF's list of moves](https://rsssf.org/tablesm/mexall.html) shows:

- **2016-17:** Chiapas relegated, Lobos BUAP promoted. The last normal swap.
- **2017-18:** Lobos BUAP finished bottom of the relegation table but stayed, because second-division side Cafetaleros were not certified. Lobos paid US$6 million.
- **2018-19:** Veracruz finished bottom and stayed, paying US$6 million. Atlético de San Luis came up, the last club promoted on the pitch.
- **2019-20:** Veracruz were expelled after the Apertura 2019, so no one was relegated, and the Clausura 2020 was abandoned because of Covid-19.

## The 2020 suspension

On 17 April 2020 Liga MX president Enrique Bonilla [announced](https://www.mlssoccer.com/post/2020/04/17/liga-mx-president-enrique-bonilla-announces-prorel-hiatus-other-changes-league) that promotion and relegation would be suspended for the next five seasons. The 18 Liga MX owners voted for it remotely, and the second-division clubs had approved it a few days earlier, [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/soccer/2020/04/20/mexico-suspends-promotion-relegation-liga-mx-ascenso) reported.

Bonilla blamed the state of the second division, made worse by the pandemic: "several current projects (clubs) are close to bankruptcy and in danger of disappearing." The 12 Ascenso MX clubs were to become a development league, each receiving about US$845,000 a year for the five seasons. It was the first time since 1951-52 that no team would move between the divisions.

Ascenso MX was replaced by the Liga de Expansión MX. In February 2022 Liga MX set six certification requirements for Expansión clubs, including stadium and infrastructure standards, which [Mexico News Daily](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/sports/mexican-soccer-pro-rel-protests/) describes as cost-prohibitive for many. In July 2022 nine Expansión clubs called the process "nothing more than a legal simulation to disguise that there is no real commitment".

### Fines instead of relegation

Without relegation, the bottom of the relegation table still cost money. The last three clubs paid fines that went to the Liga de Expansión, [Excélsior](https://www.excelsior.com.mx/deportes/multa-tabla-cocientes-liga-mx) reported:

| Position in the relegation table | Fine |
| --- | --- |
| 18th (last) | 80 million pesos |
| 17th | 47 million pesos |
| 16th | 33 million pesos |
| Total | 160 million pesos |

[Inside World Football](https://www.insideworldfootball.com/2026/07/17/liga-mx-ends-pro-rel-for-good-breaks-ties-with-mexican-federation/) put the 80 million pesos for last place at about US$4.6 million.

## The court cases

In May 2025 ten Liga de Expansión clubs went to CAS to force an earlier return. Four later withdrew, Atlante, Cimarrones de Sonora, Alebrijes de Oaxaca and Jaiba Brava, and six continued: Atlético La Paz, Atlético Morelia, Cancún FC, Mineros de Zacatecas, Venados and Leones Negros, [Goal reported](https://www.goal.com/en/lists/promotion-and-relegation-liga-mx-court-of-arbitration-for-sport/bltb36749bbfc966302).

On 4 September 2025 CAS rejected their appeal. It found that the federation's decision of 29 April 2025 covered the seasons up to and including 2025-26, and that "the FMF had always determined to suspend the system until the conclusion of the 2025-2026 season". The reading across Mexico was that promotion and relegation would return in 2026-27.

A second ruling, reported at the end of December 2025, went the federation's way on the conditions. CAS upheld the certification process and a minimum net worth of US$30 million for a club seeking promotion, [El Informador](https://www.informador.mx/deportes/el-tas-resuelve-sobre-el-ascenso-y-el-descenso-en-la-liga-mx-20251230-0136.html) reported, but only one club now needed to be certified and win the title to go up, rather than the four that had previously had to be certified at the same time.

## July 2026: the door closes

The federation's competition regulations for 2026-27 were published on 15 July 2026. In the article on the relegation table they say, by agreement of the federation's executive committee, that "los Clubes que integran LIGA MX no descenderán a EXPANSIÓN MX" and "los Clubes de EXPANSIÓN MX no ascenderán a la LIGA MX": Liga MX clubs will not go down and Expansión clubs will not come up, [Sports Illustrated México](https://www.si.com/mx/liga-mx/oficial-la-liga-mx-se-queda-sin-ascenso-descenso-y-multas-2026-2027) reported. The regulations also mention no fines, so the bottom three no longer pay. At the same time Liga MX announced it would run its administration separately from the federation.

Francisco Iturbide, the Liga MX president, explained the thinking in August. He said a six-year institutional gap had opened and Expansión clubs lacked the financial strength for promotion: "No estamos preparados para eso, vamos a trabajar para estarlo" ("We are not ready for that; we are going to work to be ready"), he told [El Universal](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/deportes/la-fmf-explica-por-que-no-habra-ascenso-y-descenso/). The Liga de Expansión would take in reserve teams of Liga MX clubs, such as Tapatío and Cruz Azul Hidalgo, with a long-term aim of one for each of the 18 top-flight clubs. Centralising broadcast rights, he argued, could in time make Expansión clubs strong enough for promotion to return.

### The one way in

For now the only way into Liga MX is to take over a club already in it. Atlante, relegated in the Clausura 2014, are back for 2026-27 because their owner, Emilio Escalante, bought Mazatlán FC's place. Liga MX's owners [authorised the sale](https://www.clarosports.com/futbol/liga-mx/atlante-regresa-a-primera-division-la-liga-mx-autoriza-la-venta-del-mazatlan-fc/) in December 2025. Atlante started the Apertura 2026 at the bottom of the relegation table with a cociente of zero, a table that no longer sends anyone down.

## The fightback

The reaction in the second division was immediate. At the start of the Expansión season several teams refused to play for the first minute, passing the ball among themselves while their opponents stood still; players covered their mouths in team photos; Atlético Morelia asked fans to wear black, and federation president Mikel Arriola was booed at the Concacaf Under-20 final, [Mexico News Daily](https://mexiconewsdaily.com/sports/mexican-soccer-pro-rel-protests/) reported. The federation threatened fines of more than US$11,000 for further disruption.

In mid-August five clubs, Cancún FC, Atlético La Paz, Atlético Morelia, Leones Negros of the Universidad de Guadalajara and Club Deportivo Zacatecas, lodged a new CAS appeal against the federation and Liga MX. It is the third time Expansión clubs have taken the issue to the court, and a hearing is set for 18-19 November 2026 in Mexico City, [La Crónica](https://www.cronica.com.mx/deportes/2026/10/15/tas-fija-la-fecha-para-decidir-sobre-el-regreso-del-ascenso-y-descenso-en-liga-mx-cuando-se-dara-el-veredicto/) reported, citing CAS. A verdict need not follow straight away.

Politics has joined in. At a Senate forum on 21 September 2026, Clemente Castañeda, who leads Movimiento Ciudadano's senators, said the party would push a bill writing sporting merit into the obligations of Mexico's sports associations, [Expansión Política](https://politica.expansion.mx/mexico/2026/09/21/senado-iniciativa-regreso-ascenso-descenso-futbol-mexicano) reported. Arriola and Iturbide were invited and did not attend. Leones Negros president Alberto Castellanos described the clubs' position: "si sacábamos la pistola yendo al TAS, ellos nos iban a sacar los tanques" ("if we drew a pistol by going to CAS, they were going to bring out the tanks"). The same report noted that Mexico's competition authority had opened an investigation into possible monopolistic practices in Mexican football the week before.

## Mexico and the leagues around it

The model Liga MX now has, a fixed membership that new owners buy into, is the model of Major League Soccer in the United States and Canada. The leagues of England, Spain, Germany, Italy and France all still promote and relegate. Germany protects its clubs from takeover in a different way, through members' votes rather than closed doors, as [the 50+1 rule](/guides/50-plus-1-rule-bundesliga) explains, and Spain's biggest club keeps outside owners out by its own route, set out in [who owns Real Madrid](/guides/who-owns-real-madrid). How points and positions decide a normal league table is covered in [how league tables and points work](/guides/how-league-tables-and-points-work).
