**Guardiola's Barcelona of 2008-12 have the strongest claim of any modern club side to be the best team of all time: 14 trophies from a possible 19 in four seasons, six of them in 2009 alone, two Champions Leagues and three straight La Liga titles.** The case against is short but serious. They never retained the European Cup, which Real Madrid, Ajax and Milan all did.

This page judges them on four things: trophies, dominance over their rivals, the way they played, and the players they produced. It sets out the numbers season by season, then puts them beside the other sides usually named in the same argument.

### 2008-09: the treble

Guardiola was promoted from the B team, which he had just taken up a division, and handed a side coming off two seasons without a trophy. At his first Joan Gamper Trophy he told the crowd: "I can't promise you silverware, but I can say that we'll keep on battling to the end and you'll be proud of us."

The league season began with a 1-0 defeat at Numancia and a 1-1 draw with Racing Santander. The result that defined the season came on 2 May 2009, when Barcelona won 6-2 at the Santiago Bernabéu, with two goals each from Thierry Henry and Lionel Messi and one each from Carles Puyol and Gerard Piqué. [Spain's Público](https://www.publico.es/deportes/estigma-2-6.html) headlined it as Real Madrid's "estigma", its stigma.

The Copa del Rey was won 4-1 against Athletic Club, the club's first cup since 1998, and the league title followed. On 27 May 2009 in Rome, Barcelona beat the holders Manchester United 2-0 in the [Champions League final](https://www.uefa.com/news/newsid=833537.html): Samuel Eto'o turned past Nemanja Vidić to score after ten minutes, and Messi headed in Xavi's cross on 70. Barcelona became the first Spanish side to win the league, the cup and the European Cup in one season, and Guardiola, at 38, the sixth man to lift the European Cup as a player and as a coach.

### 2009: six in a year

The treble turned into a sextuple in the second half of 2009. Barcelona beat Athletic Club again in the Spanish Super Cup, then Shakhtar Donetsk in the UEFA Super Cup in Monaco, where the substitute Pedro scored the winner with about five minutes left. In the [Club World Cup final in Abu Dhabi](https://www.fcbarcelona.com/en/news/1537648/ten-years-since-historic-sextuple) on 19 December, they were losing to Estudiantes until Pedro equalised late; Messi won it in extra time with his chest.

It was the first time a club had won six trophies in a single calendar year.

### 2009-10: 99 points

The European run ended in the semi-final against José Mourinho's Inter, but the league defence set a points total of 99, a record at the time, with 31 wins and a single defeat. The title was settled on the last day, 4-0 against Valladolid at Camp Nou: the first time since 1993-94 that Barcelona had clinched the league in front of their own fans. Messi scored 34 league goals.

### 2010-11: the peak

By 2010-11 Mourinho was at Real Madrid, and on 29 November 2010 Barcelona beat his side 5-0 at Camp Nou. [The AP report](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/sports/2010/11/30/barcelona-rout-real-madrid-5-0) lists the goals: Xavi after ten minutes, Pedro, David Villa twice early in the second half and the substitute Jeffren in the 90th. It was Barcelona's fifth straight Clásico win and the heaviest defeat of Mourinho's career; he had never lost by more than three goals.

Real Madrid beat them 1-0 in the Copa del Rey final, Guardiola's first defeat in a final, but Barcelona won the league with 96 points and knocked Madrid out of the Champions League semi-finals. The final at Wembley on 28 May 2011 is the centrepiece of the case for this team.

He added: "They mesmerise you with their passing and we never really controlled Messi." Guardiola would not make the claim for his own side: "It's impossible to say as I didn't see the Madrid of Di Stefano, the Santos of Pele or the Ajax of Cruyff."

## How they played

Guardiola used a 4-3-3, the same shape he had played in himself as a Barcelona player. Its distinctive feature was the centre-forward who was not one.

**The false 9.** According to [StatsBomb's analysis](https://blogarchive.statsbomb.com/articles/soccer/messi-moments-with-alex-delmas-real-madrid-2-6-barcelona-may-2009/) of the 6-2 at the Bernabéu, that game was the first time Guardiola moved Messi into the middle: he started on the right and swapped with Eto'o about seven minutes in. The role, and why it is so hard to mark, is covered in [types of striker explained](/guides/types-of-striker-explained).

**The midfield three.** Sergio Busquets, Xavi and Andrés Iniesta made up the midfield in the 2011 final, and Xavi and Iniesta, like Messi, came through the club's La Masia academy. The [4-3-3 formation explained](/guides/4-3-3-formation-explained) sets out how the holding midfielder and the two interiors share the work in this shape.

**The academy.** On 10 January 2011 the Ballon d'Or podium was Messi, Iniesta and Xavi, in that order. [Barcelona note](https://www.fcbarcelona.com/en/news/1924303/10-years-since-a-unique-ballon-dor-podium) that it is the only time three players trained in the same club's academy have filled all three places.

## The final season

2011-12 was the most productive year of Messi's career: [73 goals in 60 games](https://theanalyst.com/articles/lionel-messi-barcelona-73-goal-2011-12-season/) in all competitions, including five against Bayer Leverkusen, the first five-goal game in the Champions League. His 50 league goals in 37 games are still the [La Liga record for a season](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/most-goals-scored-in-a-season-in-the-top-division-of-spanish-football).

The team added four more trophies, including a 4-0 win over Neymar's Santos in the [Club World Cup final](https://www.fcbarcelona.com/en/news/1146967/one-year-ago-today-fc-barcelona-won-the-club-world-cup-in-japan) in Yokohama. But Real Madrid won the league with 100 points to Barcelona's 91, and Chelsea knocked them out of the Champions League semi-finals. On 27 April 2012 Guardiola announced he was leaving. "Cuatro años son muchos, el tiempo desgasta mucho," [he said](https://www.publico.es/deportes/guardiola-cede-testigo-vilanova-me-he-vaciado-necesito-rellenarme.amp.html): four years is a long time, and time wears you down. His assistant Tito Vilanova took over. Guardiola's last game was the Copa del Rey final, 3-0 against Athletic Club.

## How they compare with the other candidates

The sides usually named alongside Guardiola's Barcelona are the ones that dominated Europe. Here is the record on the one measure where they differ most:

| Team | European Cups | Won in a row? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Real Madrid, 1956-60 | 5 (1956, 1957, 1958, 1959, 1960) | Yes, five |
| Ajax, 1971-73 | 3 (1971, 1972, 1973) | Yes, three |
| Milan, 1989-90 | 2 (1989, 1990) | Yes, two |
| Real Madrid, 2016-18 | 3 (2016, 2017, 2018) | Yes, three |
| **Barcelona, 2009-11** | **2 (2009, 2011)** | **No** |

Finals as listed in the [record of European Cup and Champions League finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_European_Cup_and_UEFA_Champions_League_finals).

**The case for Barcelona.** Three straight league titles, two European Cups, the first sextuple in a calendar year and a Ballon d'Or podium made up entirely of their own academy graduates, all inside four seasons. And the "best team we have faced" verdict came from the manager they beat in both of their finals.

**The case against.** Barcelona lost two of their four Champions League semi-finals, to Inter in 2010 and Chelsea in 2012, and so never won it in successive seasons. Real Madrid's 2016-18 side did, in the same competition format. And in Guardiola's final year Real Madrid finished nine points clear of them in La Liga.

**The verdict.** On trophies alone, Real Madrid's five in a row is the stronger record. On how completely a side outplayed the best opposition of its era, Guardiola's Barcelona have no obvious rival; the Wembley final and the 5-0 against Mourinho's Madrid are the evidence. Guardiola went on to build the [Manchester City Centurions](/guides/manchester-city-centurions-100-points), and that team's record is worth setting beside this one, as is the other great treble side of the Champions League era, [Manchester United in 1999](/guides/manchester-united-1999-treble), the club Barcelona beat in both of their finals.
