The World Cup is football's biggest prize, awarded once every four years to the last team standing out of dozens of qualifiers. Only eight countries have ever lifted it, and the gap between them tells its own story about the history of the sport.

## The all-time winners' list

| Country | Titles | Years |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brazil | 5 | 1958, 1962, 1970, 1994, 2002 |
| Germany | 4 | 1954, 1974, 1990, 2014 |
| Italy | 4 | 1934, 1938, 1982, 2006 |
| Argentina | 3 | 1978, 1986, 2022 |
| France | 2 | 1998, 2018 |
| Uruguay | 2 | 1930, 1950 |
| England | 1 | 1966 |
| Spain | 1 | 2010 |

## Why Brazil still leads the way

Brazil are the only country to have played in every World Cup since the competition began in 1930, and their five titles span five different decades. It's also the only nation to win the trophy on three different continents, which is why "penta-campeões" (five-time champions) has become part of the country's football identity.

## The next tier chasing them down

Germany and Italy sit level on four titles each, with Germany the more consistent finalist of the two in the modern era. Argentina moved into outright third place after their 2022 win in Qatar, driven largely by Lionel Messi finally adding the one trophy missing from his career.

## Does hosting help you win?

Host nations have won the World Cup six times, including Uruguay, England and France, so home advantage is real but far from decisive. More than half of all titles have still been won away from home, including three of Brazil's five.
