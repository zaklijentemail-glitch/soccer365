A free agent is a player without a club, either because their contract has expired or been mutually terminated. The headline appeal is simple: no transfer fee changes hands, so any club can sign them without negotiating with a selling club.

## Why "free" doesn't mean cheap

Skipping the transfer fee doesn't mean skipping the cost. Clubs chasing a free agent often compete on wages, signing bonuses and agent fees instead, and those numbers can rival or exceed what a fee would have cost. Since there's no fee to divide up, more of the total budget tends to end up in the player's and agent's pockets.

## The six-month pre-contract window

Under FIFA rules, once a player is inside the final six months of their contract, they're free to agree terms with a foreign club and sign a pre-contract agreement, even though they're technically still registered elsewhere. The move only becomes official once the current deal expires, but the interest usually becomes public well before then.

## Why clubs let players leave for nothing

Selling a player before their contract runs out at least guarantees a fee. Letting them leave for free is usually a sign a club couldn't agree a new deal, decided the player wasn't worth extending, or simply ran out of time to find a buyer. It's one reason contract renewals are negotiated years in advance for a club's most valuable players.
