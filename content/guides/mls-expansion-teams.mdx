**MLS has added 23 expansion teams since its original ten clubs kicked off the league in 1996**, taking it to 30 clubs for the 2026 season. The first two, Chicago Fire and Miami Fusion, joined in 1998; the most recent, San Diego FC, played its first season in 2025. Below is every one, in order, with the year it joined, when it was announced, and what it paid to get in.

## How the league grew

MLS expansion has come in three distinct waves. The first ran from 1998 to 2012, adding clubs roughly every one to three years as the league found its feet: Chicago Fire and Miami Fusion (1998), Real Salt Lake and Chivas USA (2005), Toronto FC (2007), a reinstated San Jose Earthquakes (2008), Seattle Sounders FC (2009), Philadelphia Union (2010), Portland Timbers and Vancouver Whitecaps FC (2011), and the Montreal Impact, now CF Montréal (2012).

The second wave, from 2015 to 2023, is when expansion fees exploded. New York City FC and Orlando City SC joined in 2015, Atlanta United FC and Minnesota United FC in 2017, Los Angeles FC in 2018, FC Cincinnati in 2019, Inter Miami CF and Nashville SC in 2020, Austin FC in 2021, Charlotte FC in 2022 and St. Louis City SC in 2023. The third club to be announced in that run, Nashville SC, paid $150 million in December 2017; by December 2019, Charlotte FC's reported fee had more than doubled to $325 million.

San Diego FC, announced on 18 May 2023 and the subject of its own [official MLS press release](https://www.mlssoccer.com/news/major-league-soccer-awards-expansion-team-to-san-diego-x9222), is the most recent arrival. It debuted in 2025 at Snapdragon Stadium as the league's 30th club, under an ownership group led by Mohamed Mansour and the Sycuan Band of the Kumeyaay Nation, with Padres infielder Manny Machado also an investor.

## The $25 million outlier

Inter Miami CF looks out of place on that table, and the explanation is its own story. David Beckham's 2007 contract with LA Galaxy gave him an option to buy a future MLS expansion franchise for a fixed $25 million, far below the going rate. He [exercised that option on 5 February 2014](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/sports/2014/2/5/beckham-to-establish-team-in-miami), naming Miami as his target, but stadium problems delayed the formal award for four years. MLS only [confirmed the franchise on 29 January 2018](https://www.intermiamicf.com/en/post/2019/01/29/it-s-official-major-league-soccer-awards-expansion-team-miami), as the league's 25th club, one month after Nashville SC became its 24th. Inter Miami finally kicked off in 2020.

## Expansion team or relocation?

Not every "new" club in MLS history is an expansion team. Houston Dynamo began play in 2006 using the players, coaching staff and front office of the original San Jose Earthquakes, whose ownership group had walked away from the Bay Area market; no new franchise fee changed hands, so Houston is not counted among the 23 expansion clubs above. San Jose itself returned two years later, in 2008, under new ownership that did pay an expansion fee, which is why it appears in the table as a reinstated club rather than a relocation.

## The two that folded

Miami Fusion lasted four seasons before MLS's Board of Governors voted, on 8 January 2002, to shut it down along with Tampa Bay Mutiny, one of the league's original ten clubs from 1996, after the league had lost an estimated $250 million in its first five years. Chivas USA lasted longer, from 2005 to 2014, before MLS bought out its owners and replaced it with what became LAFC's expansion slot in Los Angeles. Of the 23 clubs that have paid to join MLS since 1998, only those two have gone out of business.

## What's next

San Diego FC's arrival took MLS to 30 clubs, and commissioner Don Garber said at the time there was no near-term plan to expand beyond that number. No 31st franchise has been announced since, though ownership groups continue to explore MLS in other cities, and this growth is only one side of how the league compares with soccer elsewhere: unlike [Liga MX, which has suspended its own promotion and relegation system](/guides/liga-mx-promotion-relegation-explained), MLS has always been a closed league that grows only when the league itself decides to sell a new franchise.

That business model has also pushed expansion fees well past typical transfer spending: Charlotte FC and St. Louis City SC's reported fees, at $325 million and $200 million, dwarf most clubs' entire playing budgets, and the money flowing into the league now funds rosters that include some of the [highest-paid players in MLS](/guides/highest-paid-mls-players), including stars signed well after their clubs had already paid tens or hundreds of millions just to exist.
