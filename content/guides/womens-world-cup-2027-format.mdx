**The 2027 Women's World Cup is being held in Brazil from 24 June to 25 July 2027, with 32 teams playing 64 matches in eight host cities.** The teams are drawn into eight groups of four, the top two in each group reach a round of 16, and the tournament opens and ends at the Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro.

It is the first Women's World Cup in South America, and the tournament has been played since 1991. With seven months to go, 18 of the 32 places are filled, the European and North American races finish in the next three weeks, and the last three spots go to a play-off in February.

## How Brazil won the hosting vote

The FIFA Congress picked Brazil in Bangkok on 17 May 2024, by 119 votes to 78 over a joint bid from Belgium, the Netherlands and Germany. It was the [first time all of FIFA's member associations voted](https://www.ksl.com/article/51016151/brazil-picked-by-fifa-to-get-soccers-2027-womens-world-cup-a-first-for-south-america) on the host of the women's tournament; the FIFA Council had made the decision before. The field had already shrunk: South Africa withdrew in November 2023, and the United States and Mexico pulled their joint bid in April 2024 to aim for 2031 instead.

Brazil's federation president, Ednaldo Rodrigues, put it simply on the day: "We are a South American country that achieved the victory for women's soccer."

## The dates and the calendar

The FIFA Council fixed the dates at its meeting on 10 December 2024, after consulting the Brazilian federation and the confederations. The tournament runs from a Thursday opener on 24 June to a Sunday final on 25 July, a month and a day.

Brazil's group matches are already set:

| Date | Venue | City |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thursday 24 June | Estádio do Maracanã | Rio de Janeiro |
| Tuesday 29 June | Arena Itaquera | São Paulo |
| Sunday 4 July | Estádio Nacional | Brasília |

As hosts, Brazil are the top seed in Group A. Their opponents will be known after the draw on 11 December, after which FIFA will publish an updated schedule with group-stage kick-off times.

## The eight host cities

FIFA [unveiled the match schedule](https://inside.fifa.com/en/news/womens-world-cup-2027-brazil-maracana-match-schedule-unveiled) on 20 August 2026. Every host city stages at least seven matches and at least one knockout match, but how far into the tournament each one goes varies.

| City | Furthest round it hosts |
| --- | --- |
| Rio de Janeiro | Final (plus a semi-final and the opener) |
| São Paulo | Semi-final and the third-place match |
| Belo Horizonte | Quarter-final |
| Fortaleza | Quarter-final |
| Brasília | Round of 16 |
| Porto Alegre | Round of 16 |
| Recife | Round of 16 |
| Salvador | Round of 16 |

The Maracanã will become only the third stadium to stage both a men's World Cup final and a Women's World Cup final. The other two are the Råsunda Stadium in Solna, Sweden, which hosted the 1958 and 1995 finals and has since been demolished, and the Rose Bowl in Pasadena, which hosted the 1994 and 1999 finals.

Dame Sarai Bareman, FIFA's Chief Women's Football Officer, said the schedule tried to balance sporting fairness across all 32 teams by weighing travel distances and recovery time. "We want the players to have the best experience and perform at their best," she said.

## The format

The shape is the same as at the 2023 World Cup in Australia and New Zealand:

1. **Group stage.** 32 teams in eight groups of four. Each team plays the other three once.
2. **Round of 16.** The top two in every group go through. There is no route for third-placed teams, unlike the men's 48-team format.
3. **Quarter-finals, semi-finals, final.** Single-elimination knockout matches, with a third-place match in São Paulo before the final in Rio.

That makes 48 group matches and 16 knockout matches, 64 in all. A team that wins the tournament plays seven times.

The difference from the men's tournament matters if you are coming to this straight after the summer. At the [48-team men's World Cup in 2026](/guides/world-cup-2026-format-explained), eight of the 12 third-placed teams survived the group stage. In Brazil, half the field goes home after three games, so a bad opening result is genuinely dangerous. Our guide to [how league tables and points work](/guides/how-league-tables-and-points-work) covers how small margins separate teams in a short group.

## How the 32 places are shared out

The FIFA Council [approved the slot allocation](https://inside.fifa.com/news/fifa-womens-world-cup-brazil-2027-dates-confirmed) at the same December 2024 meeting. Twenty-nine places go directly to teams through their confederations, including Brazil, and three are decided in an inter-confederation play-off.

| Confederation | Direct places | Play-off places |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UEFA (Europe) | 11 | 1 |
| AFC (Asia) | 6 | 2 |
| CAF (Africa) | 4 | 2 |
| Concacaf (North and Central America, Caribbean) | 4 | 2 |
| CONMEBOL (South America) | 3, including hosts Brazil | 2 |
| OFC (Oceania) | 1 | 1 |
| **Total** | **29** | **10 teams for 3 places** |

## Who has qualified so far

As of 16 November 2026, 18 teams are confirmed.

| Confederation | Qualified | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hosts | Brazil | Automatic, 17 May 2024 |
| AFC | Australia, China, Japan, South Korea | Reached the 2026 Women's Asian Cup semi-finals |
| AFC | North Korea, Philippines | Won play-in matches between beaten quarter-finalists |
| OFC | New Zealand | Beat Papua New Guinea 1-0 in the Oceania final, April 2026 |
| CONMEBOL | Colombia, Argentina | First and second in the Women's Nations League, June 2026 |
| UEFA | Denmark, France, Germany, Spain | Won their League A qualifying groups, June 2026 |
| CAF | Morocco, Algeria, Cameroon, Malawi | Reached the 2026 Women's Africa Cup of Nations semi-finals |

**Asia.** Japan beat Australia 1-0 in the [Asian Cup final in Sydney](https://inside.fifa.com/news/japan-afc-womens-asia-cup-1-0-australia-matthias-grafstrom) on 21 March 2026, but all four semi-finalists had already booked their places. The Philippines then beat Uzbekistan 2-0 on the Gold Coast and North Korea beat Chinese Taipei 4-0 to take the last two direct spots.

**Africa.** The WAFCON quarter-finals in Morocco on 8 and 9 August produced the biggest stories of qualifying so far. Algeria came from behind to beat Ivory Coast 2-1 and reach a Women's World Cup for the first time. Malawi, ranked 153rd in the world before the tournament, beat Ghana 2-1 to qualify for the first time as well. Cameroon knocked out the holders Nigeria 1-0.

Nigeria then lost 2-1 to South Africa in a play-off on 13 August and will [miss a Women's World Cup for the first time](https://www.myjoyonline.com/nigeria-to-miss-womens-world-cup-after-south-africa-and-ghana-win-play-offs/). They had played at all nine previous tournaments, going back to China in 1991.

**South America.** Colombia won the first CONMEBOL Women's Nations League on 9 June 2026 and Argentina finished second, taking the two direct places alongside Brazil. [Ecuador and Venezuela](https://copaamerica.com/en/news/south-american-teams-qualified-fifa-womens-world-cup-brazil-2027), third and fourth, went into the play-off tournament.

## What is still to be decided

Fourteen places are open, and most of them will be filled before the draw.

**Concacaf: four places, 27 November to 5 December 2026.** The [Concacaf W Championship](https://www.concacaf.com/competitions/w-championship/news/2026-concacaf-w-championship-schedule-confirmed-tickets-now-on-sale) is an eight-team knockout in Texas. The quarter-finals on 27 and 28 November are the World Cup qualifiers: the United States against El Salvador, Canada against Panama, Mexico against Haiti and Jamaica against Costa Rica. The four winners go to Brazil. The four losers meet in play-in matches on 2 December, and the two winners go into the inter-confederation play-off.

**UEFA: seven places, 1 and 5 December 2026.** Eight two-legged play-off ties decide Europe's remaining places. The [second-round ties](https://www.uefa.com/womenseuropeanqualifiers/news/02a6-20e5c80e2800-0f6b47898506-1000--women-s-european-qualifiers-for-2027-fifa-women-s-world-cup-/) are:

- Ukraine v England
- Finland v Italy
- Portugal v Iceland
- Wales v Norway
- Switzerland v Austria
- Scotland v Sweden
- Poland v Republic of Ireland
- Slovenia v Netherlands

The seven winners with the best overall ranking from the qualifying leagues go straight to Brazil. The lowest-ranked winner goes into the February play-off, so winning a tie is not quite enough on its own.

**The inter-confederation play-off: three places.** Ten teams compete in two phases.

- **Preliminary phase, 28 November to 5 December 2026, Rabat.** [Six teams](https://inside.fifa.com/en/news/rabat-womens-world-cup-2027-play-off-tournament-preliminary-phase) play three matches each at the Mohammed VI Football Complex: Chinese Taipei, Ecuador, Ghana, Papua New Guinea, South Africa and Uzbekistan. The top two go through.
- **Final phase, February 2027.** Those two join Concacaf's two play-in winners, the top-ranked CONMEBOL play-off team, Venezuela, and Europe's lowest-ranked play-off winner. The six are drawn into three knockout paths, with teams from the same confederation kept apart, and the three path winners qualify.

## The holders and the last 32-team edition

Spain arrive as champions. They beat England 1-0 in the 2023 final at Stadium Australia in Sydney, in front of 75,784, with Olga Carmona scoring in the 29th minute. It was Spain's first Women's World Cup title, and they have already qualified for Brazil by winning their League A group.

Brazil 2027 should be the last Women's World Cup with 32 teams. The 2031 tournament has been [expanded to 48](https://www.ksl.com/article/51645328/fifa-releases-evaluation-reports-for-the-2031-and-2035-womens-world-cup), and a joint bid by the United States, Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica is due to go to a vote at an Extraordinary FIFA Congress on 23 November 2026, alongside a bid from England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland for 2035.

For fans who want to go, FIFA has opened registration of interest for tickets at FIFA.com/tickets. How level knockout matches are settled in general is set out in our guide to [extra time and penalty shootouts](/guides/extra-time-and-penalty-shootouts-explained).
