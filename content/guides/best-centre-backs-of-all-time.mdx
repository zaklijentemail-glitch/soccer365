**The best centre-back of all time is Franz Beckenbauer**, the sweeper who won two Ballons d'Or, captained West Germany to the 1974 World Cup and won three straight European Cups with Bayern Munich, with Franco Baresi and Bobby Moore next. The full top ten, with the numbers behind each pick, follows.

## How the list is ranked

Four things, in this order:

1. **Major honours.** World Cups, continental championships, European Cups and Champions Leagues and league titles, with more weight on the ones a player was central to.
2. **Longevity at the top level.** Seasons at a major club and caps won for a leading national team.
3. **Individual recognition.** Ballon d'Or finishes, FIFPro World XI places and similar selections.
4. **What they did to the position.** Some of these players changed what a centre-back was expected to do.

A player is judged on the years he spent in central defence, including as a sweeper. Appearance totals come from the club, league or other sources linked in each entry; where sources disagree, the official figure is used or the number is left out. What a centre-back does, and how the job has changed, is set out in [the centre-back role explained](/guides/centre-back-role-explained).

## 1. Franz Beckenbauer (West Germany, Bayern Munich)

**Career:** 424 Bundesliga games, 396 for Bayern and 28 for Hamburg, 44 goals; 103 West Germany caps, 14 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 1974, Euro 1972, European Cup 1974, 1975 and 1976, five Bundesliga titles, Ballon d'Or 1972 and 1976.

Beckenbauer is often credited with inventing the modern libero, the free defender who sweeps behind the back line and then steps out with the ball, and FIFA calls him [one of the best liberos the world has ever seen](https://inside.fifa.com/news/der-kaiser-turns-75). He is one of only two centre-backs to win the Ballon d'Or, and the only one to win it twice. He also finished second in 1974 and 1975.

He captained West Germany to the 1974 World Cup on home soil, two years after winning the European Championship, and Bayern won the European Cup in 1974, 1975 and 1976. Four of his five league titles came at Bayern, the last at Hamburg in 1982. According to the [Bundesliga's record of his career](https://www.bundesliga.com/en/bundesliga/news/franz-beckenbauer-career-in-numbers-bayern-munich-germany-25884), he was never sent off and received only four yellow cards. He then won the 1990 World Cup as Germany's coach, one of three men to win it as both player and manager. He died in January 2024, aged 78.

## 2. Franco Baresi (Italy, AC Milan)

**Career:** 719 official Milan games, 33 goals, 532 of them in Serie A; 81 Italy caps, 1 goal. **Honours:** European Cup 1989, 1990 and 1994, six Serie A titles, two Intercontinental Cups, World Cup 1982 (squad).

Baresi played for one club, and [Milan's own record](https://acmilan.com/en/club/legends/players/baresi) gives the scale of it: 719 official matches between 1977 and 1997, six league titles and three European Cups, captaining the side under Arrigo Sacchi and Fabio Capello. He was captain for 15 seasons and finished second in the 1989 Ballon d'Or.

For Italy he was in the squad that won the 1982 World Cup and captained the side to the 1994 final, where, the [Irish Examiner recalled](https://www.irishexaminer.com/sport/soccer/arid-41888830.html), he "played the full 120 minutes just 25 days after knee surgery" before Brazil won on penalties. Milan retired his No.6 shirt, the [first player in the club's history](https://www.thestar.com.my/sport/football/2026/07/31/former-ac-milan-and-italy-defender-baresi-dead-at-66) to receive that honour. He died in July 2026, aged 66.

## 3. Bobby Moore (England, West Ham)

**Career:** 544 league games for West Ham; 108 England caps, 2 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 1966, Cup Winners' Cup 1965, FA Cup 1964.

Moore lifted the World Cup at Wembley in 1966, and his statue there calls him the "first Englishman to raise the World Cup aloft". [England's own profile](https://www.englandfootball.com/england/mens-senior-team/squad/Legends-profiles/Bobby-Moore) records 108 caps between 1962 and 1973 and 90 games as captain, a record he shares with Billy Wright.

At West Ham he lifted the FA Cup in 1964 and the European Cup Winners' Cup in 1965, both as captain, and FIFA notes he went on to [break the club's appearance record](https://inside.fifa.com/news/remembering-bobby-moore-2017217). He finished second in the 1970 Ballon d'Or. He died in 1993, aged 51, from bowel cancer.

## 4. Fabio Cannavaro (Italy, Parma, Juventus and Real Madrid)

**Career:** 212 Serie A games for Parma; 136 Italy caps, 2 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 2006, UEFA Cup 1999, Coppa Italia 1999 and 2002, La Liga 2007 and 2008, Ballon d'Or 2006.

Cannavaro is the most recent defender to win the Ballon d'Or. In 2006 he captained Italy to the World Cup in Germany, [winning his 100th cap in the final](https://www.uefa.com/news/01ed-0ea28499bbcb-ecf590e93e22-1000--fabio-cannavaro/) against France, and that year he also became FIFA World Player of the Year. His 136 caps were an Italy record.

At Parma he won the UEFA Cup and two Coppa Italias. He won two league titles with Juventus, both later revoked in the Calciopoli scandal, then two with Real Madrid.

## 5. Sergio Ramos (Spain, Real Madrid)

**Career:** 671 Real Madrid games, 101 goals; 180 Spain caps, 23 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 2010, Euro 2008 and 2012, four Champions Leagues, five La Liga titles.

Ramos is Spain's most-capped player, with 180 games, and in November 2020 [became the most-capped male European player](https://global.espn.com/football/story/_/id/37608715/spain-real-madrid-captain-ramos-becomes-most-capped-european-player-ever) at the time. At Real Madrid he won [22 trophies in 16 years](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2021/6/16/real-madrid-captain-sergio-ramos-to-leave-the-club) and scored 101 goals, and he was captain from 2015.

The goal that defines him came in the 2014 Champions League final: a header from Luka Modrić's corner in the third minute of stoppage time to equalise against Atlético Madrid, before Real won 4-1 after extra time. He was named in the FIFPro World XI 11 times.

## 6. Gaetano Scirea (Italy, Juventus)

**Career:** 552 Juventus games; 78 Italy caps, 2 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 1982, European Cup 1985, Cup Winners' Cup 1984, UEFA Cup 1977, seven Serie A titles.

Scirea was Juventus's sweeper. Over 14 seasons at Juventus he won [seven league titles](https://www.juventus.com/it/news/articoli/gaetano-scirea-sempre-con-noi) and the European Cup, Cup Winners' Cup and UEFA Cup, which makes him one of the few players to win all of UEFA's major club competitions. With Italy he won the 1982 World Cup alongside his friend and Italy captain Dino Zoff.

Juventus point out that he did all of it without ever being sent off. He died on 3 September 1989 in a car crash in Poland, aged 36.

## 7. Daniel Passarella (Argentina, River Plate)

**Career:** 70 Argentina caps, 22 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 1978, seven Argentine league titles with River Plate.

Passarella captained Argentina to the World Cup at home in 1978. César Luis Menotti, the coach, [told FIFA](https://inside.fifa.com/es/news/origin1904-p.cxm.fifa.com/passarella-el-gran-capitan) he "never hesitated" in giving him the armband, and Passarella led the side in all seven games through to the 3-1 win over the Netherlands in the final.

He was a centre-back who scored like a midfielder. FIFA credits him with 99 goals and calls him the highest-scoring centre-back in the history of Argentine football, and his 22 international goals in 70 games, [recorded by RSSSF](https://rsssf.org/miscellaneous/passarella-intlg.html), are an unusual return for a defender. After River Plate he played for Fiorentina and Inter.

## 8. Carles Puyol (Spain, Barcelona)

**Career:** 593 competitive Barcelona games; 100 Spain caps, 3 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 2010, Euro 2008, three Champions Leagues, six La Liga titles.

Puyol played for one club and [won 21 titles there](https://fcbarcelona.com/en/football/first-team/news/2645648/25-years-since-carles-puyols-debut), captaining Barcelona to three Champions Leagues. He lifted the trophy himself in 2006 and 2009 and handed it to Éric Abidal at Wembley in 2011.

His most famous moment came in the 2010 World Cup semi-final in Durban: Xavi's corner, Puyol's header in the [73rd minute](https://mlssoccer.com/news/spain-edge-past-germany-late-puyol-goal), and a 1-0 win over Germany that put Spain into the final. He was in the FIFPro World XI three times.

## 9. Alessandro Nesta (Italy, Lazio and AC Milan)

**Career:** 193 Serie A games for Lazio and 224 for Milan; 78 Italy caps. **Honours:** World Cup 2006, Champions League 2003 and 2007, Serie A 2000, 2004 and 2011, Cup Winners' Cup 1999.

Nesta became captain of his home-town club, winning the 2000 Scudetto and the [1999 Cup Winners' Cup](https://figc.it/en/fans/news/happy-birthday-to-alessandro-nesta-who-turns-45). At Milan he won two Champions Leagues and two more league titles. He was named Serie A Defender of the Year four years in a row, from 2000 to 2003.

The one gap is the 2006 World Cup: he won it, but a groin injury in the group game against the Czech Republic kept him out of the rest of the tournament.

## 10. Elías Figueroa (Chile, Peñarol and Internacional)

**Career:** 47 Chile caps, three World Cups (1966, 1974 and 1982). **Honours:** Uruguayan league 1967 and 1968 with Peñarol, Brazilian league 1975 and 1976 with Internacional, South American Footballer of the Year 1974, 1975 and 1976.

Figueroa is the only player in the ten who never played club football in Europe. He was voted South American Footballer of the Year three years running while at Internacional, and he scored the goal that won the club's first Brazilian title in 1975, known in Porto Alegre as the "gol iluminado", [La Tercera recalls](https://www.latercera.com/el-deportivo/noticia/uno-crece-como-hincha-escuchando-leyendas-de-figueroa-el-amor-eterno-entre-don-elias-e-inter-de-porto-alegre/YRU445BH4NFPXO7C2VOSXQ7SVU/). In 2006 he was [named the best player in Chilean football history](https://cooperativa.cl/noticias/deportes/futbol/elias-figueroa/elias-figueroa-fue-distinguido-como-el-mejor-jugador-de-la-historia-de/2006-11-11/082828.html).

## Close calls

**Virgil van Dijk (Netherlands, Liverpool).** The best centre-back of his generation, and still playing. In 2019 he became the [first defender to win UEFA's Men's Player of the Year](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/588787-first-defender-to-win-uefa-mens-player-of-the-year-award) and finished [second in the Ballon d'Or](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/12040/11876624/lionel-messi-crowned-2019-ballon-dor-winner). He has won the Champions League and two Premier League titles with Liverpool, 387 games and 36 goals according to [LFChistory](https://www.lfchistory.net/players/player/profile/1321), and 100 caps for the Netherlands.

**John Terry (England, Chelsea).** Five Premier League titles and the 2012 Champions League. Sky Sports' report of his Hall of Fame induction lists him as the league's [top-scoring defender, with 41 goals](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/12040/13121141/andy-cole-and-john-terry-inducted-into-premier-league-hall-of-fame), and the defender with the most clean sheets, 214. He won 78 England caps.

**Paolo Maldini (Italy, AC Milan).** He moved to centre-back in the second half of his career, but he started at left-back and is ranked first on our list of the [best left-backs of all time](/guides/best-left-backs-of-all-time).

## How the centre-back has changed

The list traces the position's history. Beckenbauer and Scirea played as sweepers, the free defender who covers behind the line and carries the ball out of it. Moore, Baresi and Cannavaro captained their countries to World Cups or World Cup finals from the middle of the defence. Passarella and Ramos showed that a centre-back could also be a regular goalscorer, with 99 and 101 goals by the counts above. Van Dijk's era asks for all of it at once.
