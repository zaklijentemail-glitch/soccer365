Every football table looks like a wall of confusing numbers at first. This guide breaks it down piece by piece, so you can glance at any table and immediately understand who's doing well and why.

## The basic idea: a season-long competition

A league season is simply every team playing every other team, usually twice: once at home, once away. Every match played earns a team points, based on the result.

- **Win** = 3 points
- **Draw** = 1 point each
- **Loss** = 0 points

At the end of the season, whichever team has collected the most points is the champion. It really is that simple underneath all the numbers.

## Reading a table, column by column

A league table lists every team from best to worst, ranked by points. Alongside the team name, you'll usually see a row of short labels:

- **P (Played)** – How many matches the team has played so far.
- **W / D / L (Win / Draw / Loss)** – How many of those matches they won, drew, or lost.
- **GF / GA (Goals For / Goals Against)** – How many goals they've scored, and how many they've let in.
- **GD (Goal Difference)** – Goals For minus Goals Against. A big positive number means a team scores a lot and defends well.
- **Pts (Points)** – Their total points, which decides their position in the table.

## What if two teams have the same points?

This is where goal difference comes in. If Team A and Team B both have 40 points, the team with the better goal difference (say, +20 instead of +10) is ranked higher.

Think of goal difference as a measure of how convincingly a team wins and how rarely it gets beaten badly.

## Moving up and down: promotion and relegation

Most football pyramids have several divisions stacked on top of each other, like floors in a building.

- The top few teams in a lower division get **promoted** to the division above next season.
- The bottom few teams in a higher division get **relegated** down to the division below.

This keeps every single match meaningful, even matches between two mid-table teams, since climbing or dropping even a few places can decide who goes up or down.

## Leagues vs. cups vs. groups-then-knockouts

Not every competition works like a season-long league. There are two other common formats:

- **Knockout cups** – Lose once, and you're out. Two teams play, and only the winner moves on to the next round.
- **Group stage + knockouts** – Common in major tournaments. Teams are split into small groups and play everyone in their group, earning points just like a mini-league. The top teams from each group then move into a knockout stage, where one loss ends the run.

Groups exist so that one bad day doesn't eliminate a team immediately, while the knockout stage at the end still gives every match must-win drama.
