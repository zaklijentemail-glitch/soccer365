**A regista is a playmaker who directs the game from in front of the defence, a mezzala is a midfielder who plays wider and higher to attack the half-spaces, and a carrilero is a shuttler who covers the side of midfield so others can attack.** All three live in a midfield three or a diamond. What separates them is height, freedom and how much running each does for the others.

Two of the names are Italian and one Spanish, and one of them means something different depending on which language you read it in. The table and diagrams below lay out where each stands and what each is asked to do.

## Three names, three jobs

**Regista.** The [Treccani dictionary](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/regista/) gives the word its first meaning as a theatre or film director. In football it defines the regista as a player with a well-developed tactical sense and precise passing who organises the team's play, in attack and in covering. [Coaches' Voice](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/what-is-a-regista-explained-jorginho-andrea-pirlo/) puts it simply: a creative player who operates in front of the defence, almost always centrally, and who is literally the director of the match. English calls the same player a deep-lying playmaker.

**Mezzala.** From *mezza*, half, and *ala*, wing, which is why it is usually translated as "half-winger". [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/mezzala/) also gives *interno*, inside player, because the mezzala stands more centrally than the wingers, and it describes a player mainly busy building attacks, linking defenders and forwards. It even splits the role in two: a *mezzala arretrata*, deeper, with blocking and support duties, and a *mezzala di punta*, further forward and often a goalscorer. [Guide to Football](https://guidetofootball.com/tactics/central-midfielder-roles/) gives the modern sense: "an attacking central midfielder who drifts wide into the half-spaces", receiving between the centre and the wing and arriving in advanced areas.

**Carrilero.** From the Spanish *carril*, a track or lane, plus the agent suffix *-ero*, as [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/carrilero) sets out. In English the word names a central midfielder who, in Guide to Football's [glossary](https://guidetofootball.com/glossary/positions-and-roles/), "moves across the side of midfield to support wide areas", links play and gives defensive cover, especially in narrow formations. It is the least glamorous of the three and, for many sides, the one that lets the other two function.

## The roles compared

| | Regista | Mezzala | Carrilero |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Language | Italian, "director" | Italian, "half-wing" | Spanish, from *carril*, "lane" |
| Where he stands | Centrally, in front of the back line | Wide of centre and high, in a half-space | Wide of centre, level with or behind the ball |
| Usual shapes | 4-3-3, 3-5-2, 4-4-2 diamond | 4-3-3, 3-5-2 | 4-4-2 diamond, midfield three |
| Main job with the ball | Receive from defenders, set the tempo, play forward | Combine with wide players, run into the box | Circulate, support the flank, keep the team connected |
| Main job without it | Screen the back line, cut passes to strikers | Press, track runners | Cover the full-back's space, hold the shape |
| Risk | Man-marked, or caught for pace | Leaves space behind when he goes | Little attacking output |
| Reference players | Monti, Pirlo, Jorginho, Çalhanoğlu | Barella, Modrić | The No.8s either side of a diamond |

## The regista: directing from deep

The role is older than the word suggests. According to [These Football Times](https://thesefootballtimes.co/2015/04/19/the-relationship-between-age-and-the-regista/), Vittorio Pozzo adapted the 2-3-5 for Italy while refusing to add a third back, and wanted his centre-half to distribute the ball like Charlie Roberts, the Manchester United centre-half he admired. He wanted, in the article's words, "a director, or in Italian, a regista". He found one in Luis Monti, who had played for Argentina before joining Juventus: in his thirties, overweight and short of pace, "but he was just what Pozzo wanted". With a 33-year-old Monti at its heart, Italy won the 1934 World Cup.

That detail explains why the role has always suited older players. The regista does not need to cover the most ground; the players around him do that, and his job is to see the game and move the ball into the space his team-mates create.

**Andrea Pirlo** is the modern archetype. He grew up as a trequartista, the creative player behind the strikers covered in our guide to the [No.10, the enganche and the trequartista](/guides/number-10-enganche-trequartista), but [Gulf Times](https://www.gulf-times.com/story/184760/pirlo-transformed-by-ancelotti-masterstroke) notes he never became a regular in that role at Inter, partly for lack of pace. On loan at Brescia in January 2001, [Sky Sport Italia](https://sport.sky.it/calcio/nazionale/andrea-pirlo-carriera-ct-nazionale) records, Carlo Mazzone moved him back in front of the defence so he could play alongside Roberto Baggio. Milan signed him that summer, and in the next pre-season Carlo Ancelotti tried him as a deep playmaker, first in a warm-up game and then against Juventus in the Trofeo Berlusconi. It stuck. Milan won the Coppa Italia and the Champions League in 2003, the Scudetto in 2004 and another Champions League in 2007, and Pirlo played the same role for Italy at the 2006 World Cup.

Coaches' Voice calls him "arguably as close to the perfect regista as there has ever been", and its list of examples shows how far the role has travelled since: Xavi for Spain under Luis Aragonés, Michael Carrick at the end of Sir Alex Ferguson's time at Manchester United, Xabi Alonso at Bayern under Pep Guardiola, and Jorginho, who flourished in Maurizio Sarri's 4-3-3 at Napoli and won the 2020-21 Champions League with Chelsea and Euro 2020 with Italy.

**Hakan Çalhanoğlu** is the clearest current case of a player converted into the role. [FotMob](https://www.fotmob.com/en-GB/topnews/9798-hakan-calhanoglu-proving-himself-inters-first-choice-regista) notes he was mainly used as an attacking midfielder after joining Inter in 2021, the most advanced of a midfield trio. When Marcelo Brozović was injured in September 2022, Simone Inzaghi moved him back to cover. Brozović never won the place back and was sold to Al-Nassr the following summer.

**With the ball**, Coaches' Voice says, the regista takes passes from the defenders or goalkeeper under pressure from any direction, and his main aim is to progress play with accurate vertical passes, long and short, switching play and breaking lines. **Without it**, he retreats straight to a central position, screens the back line and intercepts passes aimed at the strikers.

**The weakness** is the flip side. Registas are usually possession players who can lack defensive reading, power or pace, so they typically need a ball-winner beside them, and a team that leans on one can lose most of its threat if he is man-marked. The specialist destroyers are covered in our ranking of the [best defensive midfielders in the world](/guides/best-defensive-midfielders-in-the-world).

## The mezzala: the half-winger

If the regista is the deepest of a midfield three, the mezzala is the one either side of him who goes. Guide to Football's model shape is "two mezzalas on either side of a holding midfielder in a 4-3-3". They receive between the centre and the wing, combine with the winger and full-back to build overloads, make underlapping runs and arrive in the box. Football Manager, which added the role in its 2018 edition, described it as similar to a box-to-box midfielder "but with less defensive responsibility", according to [FM Scout's guide to the new roles](https://www.fmscout.com/a-fm18-new-player-roles-explained.html).

That comparison is a useful one. A [box-to-box midfielder](/guides/box-to-box-midfielder-explained) covers the full length of the pitch through the middle. A mezzala works on a diagonal: from the centre out towards the touchline and forward into the channel between the opponent's full-back and centre-back.

**Nicolò Barella** is the reference player of the past few seasons. [Total Football Analysis](https://totalfootballanalysis.com/player-analysis/nicolo-barella-2021-22-scout-report-tactical-analysis) places him on the right of Inter's 3-5-2, a system used by both Antonio Conte and Simone Inzaghi, and calls his role "what the Italians call a Mezzala". In that shape the mezzalas have even more to do, because the [3-5-2](/guides/3-5-2-formation-explained) has no wingers: they combine with the wing-backs and step out to the flank when one is caught upfield. [Jobs in Football's explainer](https://jobsinfootball.com/blog/the-mezzala-role-explained/) adds Luka Modrić on the right of Carlo Ancelotti's 4-3-3 at Real Madrid and Alexis Mac Allister on the left of Arne Slot's at Liverpool.

**The weakness** is the space a mezzala leaves. Every time he pushes into the half-space, someone has to cover the area he vacated, which is why many coaches pair one with a more disciplined player on the other side.

## The carrilero: the lane runner

That more disciplined player is often the carrilero. Guide to Football describes him as a midfielder "who shuttles between the centre and the wide area", focused on "support, circulation and protection" rather than breaking forward, "helping the team keep its shape while others attack".

His natural home is the side of a narrow diamond. The [Premier League's explainer on the 4-4-2 diamond](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4243786) describes the two midfielders either side of the single pivot as working mostly through the inside channels, providing width when the full-backs are too deep to get forward, and out of possession protecting those channels and pressing the opposing full-backs. Because a diamond has no wide midfielders, someone has to slide across when the full-back goes forward. That is the carrilero's job.

<Formation shape="4-3-1-2" roles="GK,LB,CB,CB,RB,CAR,REG,CAR,10,ST,ST" lift="REG:-0.3" caption="The carrilero's home: either side of the regista in a 4-4-2 diamond, covering the flanks the shape leaves empty" />

The diamond is also where all three roles meet most naturally. Coaches' Voice notes Pirlo did much of his best work at the base of Ancelotti's diamond at Milan, flanked by Gennaro Gattuso, Clarence Seedorf and Massimo Ambrosini. Under Massimiliano Allegri, the Premier League's explainer adds, Pirlo again played at the base of a diamond at Juventus, with Claudio Marchisio and Paul Pogba as the No.8s either side. Allegri switched from Conte's 3-5-2 to that diamond in his first season, 2014-15, retained the Serie A title and reached the Champions League final.

**A word of warning on the name.** In Spanish-language football, *carrilero* often means a wing-back, the player who runs the whole flank. [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/carrilero) gives "wingback" as the football sense and quotes a 2015 El País report calling Andrés Guardado PSV's carrilero. [Win Sports](https://www.winsports.co/futbol-internacional/noticias/la-importancia-de-los-carrileros-en-el-futbol-moderno-93583) in Colombia also uses it for a player who covers the whole flank, attacking and defending, and quotes César Luis Menotti, Argentina's 1978 World Cup-winning coach, saying carrileros would be the position of the future. The central-midfield meaning is the one English-language sources use. Football Manager added the carrilero alongside the mezzala in its 2018 edition, as "a supporting role more often than not utilised as part of a midfield three". If a Spanish report calls a player a carrilero, check whether it means the flank; the roles there are compared in our guide to the [full-back, wing-back and inverted full-back](/guides/fullback-vs-wingback-vs-inverted-fullback).

## How the three fit together

A midfield three needs, broadly, someone to start attacks, someone to finish them off and someone to protect the team while that happens. The three roles map neatly on to that.

| Combination | Shape | What it gives a team |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regista + two mezzalas | [4-3-3](/guides/4-3-3-formation-explained), 3-5-2 | Maximum attacking threat from midfield; the regista is exposed if both go |
| Regista + mezzala + carrilero | 4-3-3 | A runner on one side, a protector on the other; the most balanced three |
| Regista + two carrileros | 4-4-2 diamond | Cover for full-backs on both flanks; creativity comes from the No.10 and the regista |
| Two carrileros, no regista | Diamond with a ball-winner at the base | Solid and compact, but short of a passer to start attacks |

Coaches' Voice's examples follow that pattern. Pirlo at Milan had Gattuso's running beside him; at Juventus under Conte, he had Arturo Vidal or Marchisio and Pogba; with Italy, Gattuso and Daniele De Rossi. Jorginho at Napoli had Allan doing the defensive work, while Marek Hamšík pushed forward on the left. In each case the regista set the tempo, one partner attacked and the other covered.

## Choosing between them

A coach who wants to control games through the ball from deep needs a regista, and needs him protected. A coach who wants goals and runs from midfield, particularly with wingers who stay wide, wants a mezzala attacking the space inside them. And a coach playing narrow, in a diamond or with attacking full-backs, needs a carrilero to cover the flanks the shape leaves bare. Few elite midfields are built from one type alone; the [football formations hub](/guides/football-formations-explained) shows how each shape distributes the three.
