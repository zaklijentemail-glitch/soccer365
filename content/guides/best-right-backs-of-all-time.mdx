**The best right-back of all time is Cafu**, who won 142 caps for Brazil, lifted the World Cup as captain in 2002 and remains the only player to appear in three consecutive World Cup finals, with Philipp Lahm and Carlos Alberto next. Below is the full top ten, with the career numbers and the trophies behind each pick.

## How the list is ranked

Four things, in this order:

1. **Major honours.** League titles, European Cups and Champions Leagues, World Cups and continental championships, with more weight on the ones a player was central to.
2. **Longevity at the top level.** Seasons played at a major club and caps won for a leading national team.
3. **Individual recognition.** World Cup All-Star teams, the FIFA FIFPro World XI, the FIFA 100 and similar selections.
4. **What they did to the position.** Some of these players changed what a right-back was expected to do.

A player is judged on the years he spent at right-back. Several of the ten also played left-back, centre-back or midfield, and that is noted. Caps and appearance totals come from the club, league and other sources linked in each entry; where sources disagree, the lower or the official figure is used, or the number is left out.

## 1. Cafu (Brazil, Roma and Milan)

**Career:** 218 Roma games, 8 goals; 142 Brazil caps, 5 goals. **Honours:** World Cups 1994 and 2002, Champions League 2007, Serie A 2001 and 2004, Club World Cup 2007.

Cafu is Brazil's most-capped men's player and the only player to have appeared in three consecutive World Cup finals: he won in 1994, lost to France in 1998 and lifted the trophy as captain in 2002, after Emerson dislocated a shoulder in training. He was South American Footballer of the Year in 1994.

At Roma, where fans called him "Il Pendolino", the express train, he [played 218 games and won the 2001 Scudetto](https://www.asroma.com/en/news/58410/history-makers-cafu) before moving to Milan in 2003. There he won Serie A again in 2004 and the Champions League in 2007. Pelé named him in the FIFA 100 in 2004 and he was in the 2005 FIFPro World XI.

## 2. Philipp Lahm (Germany, Bayern Munich)

**Career:** 517 Bayern games, 16 goals; 113 Germany caps, 5 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 2014, Champions League 2013, 8 Bundesliga titles, 6 DFB-Pokals.

Lahm was a left-back first: Felix Magath converted him on loan at Stuttgart, and he was Bayern's first-choice left-back from 2006-07 until Louis van Gaal [moved him to right-back in 2009-10](https://www.bundesliga.com/en/bundesliga/news/philipp-lahm-bayern-munich-and-germany-legend-dfb-president-3430). He captained Bayern to the 2013 treble, beating Borussia Dortmund at Wembley, and captained Germany to the 2014 World Cup; the 1-0 final win over Argentina was his 113th and last international.

He was named in the World Cup All-Star team in 2006, 2010 and 2014, and in the FIFPro World XI in 2013 and 2014. Pep Guardiola, who used him in midfield, called him the most intelligent footballer he had ever coached. His 517th and final Bayern game came against Freiburg in May 2017.

## 3. Carlos Alberto (Brazil, Santos)

**Career:** more than 400 Santos games; 53 Brazil caps, 8 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 1970.

Carlos Alberto captained what is often called the greatest side in World Cup history, and he finished it off himself. Brazil won all six games in Mexico, and in the final he scored the fourth goal of a [4-1 win over Italy](https://www.tntsports.co.uk/football/brazils-1970-world-cup-winning-captain-carlos-alberto-dies-aged-72_sto5925821/story.shtml), hitting Pelé's pass first time, a strike widely regarded as one of the best in the tournament's history. The win gave Brazil the Jules Rimet Trophy to keep.

He played more than 400 games for Santos alongside Pelé and finished his playing career with the New York Cosmos in 1982. In 1998 a panel of 250 journalists picked him at full-back in a World Team of the 20th Century. He died in October 2016, aged 72.

## 4. Djalma Santos (Brazil, Palmeiras)

**Career:** Portuguesa, Palmeiras (1959-68), Atlético Paranaense; 98 Brazil caps, 3 goals. **Honours:** World Cups 1958 and 1962.

Djalma Santos played in four World Cups and was picked in the All-Star team at three of them, 1954, 1958 and 1962, a total matched only by Franz Beckenbauer and Lahm. The 1958 selection is the remarkable one: he did not play until the final, the 5-2 win over hosts Sweden, and was still named. In 1962 he started every game and set up Brazil's last goal in the final against Czechoslovakia.

His [98 official caps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djalma_Santos) span 1952 to 1968. Pelé named him in the FIFA 100, and he died in July 2013, aged 84.

## 5. Dani Alves (Brazil, Sevilla and Barcelona)

**Career:** 391 official Barcelona games, 23 goals; 126 Brazil caps, 8 goals. **Honours:** 3 Champions Leagues, 6 La Liga titles, 2 UEFA Cups, Copa América 2007 and 2019, Olympic gold 2021.

On trophies alone, nobody comes close. [Barcelona count 46 senior trophies](https://fcbarcelona.com/en/news/2349816/dani-alves-by-the-numbers/amp) in his career, 23 of them in eight seasons at the club, where he won La Liga six times and the Champions League three times. Before that he won the UEFA Cup in 2006 and 2007 and the Copa del Rey with Sevilla, and later added league titles with Juventus and PSG. Counts differ between outlets depending on which competitions are included, but he is widely reported as the most decorated player in the history of the game.

He was the first signing of the Guardiola era, presented on 2 July 2008, and returned for a second spell in 2021-22. In February 2024 he was convicted of rape by a Barcelona court and sentenced to four and a half years; in March 2025 the High Court of Justice of Catalonia [overturned the conviction](https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2025/3/28/spanish-court-overturns-ex-footballer-dani-alves-rape-conviction-on-appeal), and the following month prosecutors announced an appeal to Spain's Supreme Court.

## 6. Giuseppe Bergomi (Italy, Inter)

**Career:** 756 Inter games, 28 goals, 519 of them in Serie A; 81 Italy caps, 6 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 1982, Serie A 1989, 3 UEFA Cups, Coppa Italia 1982.

Bergomi won the World Cup at 18 and started the 1982 final against West Germany. He played his whole career for Inter, from 1979 to 1999, and only Javier Zanetti has played [more games for the club](https://www.inter.it/en/archivio_giocatore/G0057/giuseppe-bergomi). Mainly a right-back, he also played centre-back and sweeper. He won the UEFA Cup in 1991, 1994 and 1998, captained Italy 33 times, including at the 1990 World Cup, and was named in that tournament's All-Star team.

## 7. Berti Vogts (West Germany, Borussia Mönchengladbach)

**Career:** 419 Bundesliga games, 32 goals; 96 West Germany caps, 1 goal. **Honours:** World Cup 1974, Euro 1972, 5 Bundesliga titles, 2 UEFA Cups.

Vogts played only for Gladbach, from 1965 to 1979, and won five league titles in the club's best era. His defining game was the 1974 World Cup final in Munich: wearing No.2 in the [West Germany side that beat the Netherlands 2-1](https://datencenter.dfb.de/en/data-center/fifa-world-cup/1974-in-germany/final/netherlands-germany-137046), he marked Johan Cruyff and largely kept him quiet. He was in that tournament's All-Star team, and as Germany's coach he later won Euro 96.

## 8. Lilian Thuram (France, Parma and Juventus)

**Career:** 142 France caps, 2 goals. **Honours:** World Cup 1998, Euro 2000, UEFA Cup and Coppa Italia 1999, Serie A 2002 and 2003.

Thuram often played centre-back for his clubs, but for France he was the right-back, and he was picked there in the 1998 World Cup All-Star team. In the semi-final against Croatia in Paris he scored both goals in a 2-1 win, [the only two goals](https://uefa.com/european-qualifiers/news/01ac-0e6cef86ef0f-9472a3b7ceed-1000--france-prosper-from-thuram-u-turn) of his international career, and won the Bronze Ball.

His 142 caps were a France record until Hugo Lloris passed them in 2022. He retired after Euro 2004, then changed his mind a year later: "A French player cannot refuse playing for France." He went on to reach the 2006 World Cup final.

## 9. Javier Zanetti (Argentina, Inter)

**Career:** 858 Inter games, 21 goals; 145 Argentina caps. **Honours:** Champions League 2010, 5 Serie A titles, UEFA Cup 1998.

No one has played more for Inter: [858 games](https://inter.it/en/club/hall-of-fame/javier-zanetti) over 19 seasons from 1995 to 2014, 13 of them as captain. He started on the wing, played full-back or wing-back on either flank and later moved into midfield. In 2010 he became the first captain of an Italian club to win the treble, and Inter have retired his No.4 shirt. His 145 Argentina caps were a national record from 2007 to 2018.

## 10. Gary Neville (England, Manchester United)

**Career:** 602 United games, 400 in the Premier League, 148 clean sheets; 85 England caps. **Honours:** 2 Champions Leagues, 8 Premier League titles.

Neville was Sir Alex Ferguson's first-choice right-back for 12 years and club captain from 2005 to 2010. His eight titles are the [third-most in Premier League history](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4449719/gary-neville-the-premier-leagues-greatest-right-back), and in 2025 he became the first right-back inducted into its Hall of Fame. He won the Champions League in 1999, part of the [treble season](/guides/manchester-united-1999-treble), and again in 2008. For England he passed Phil Neal's record for caps by a right-back with his 51st and finished with 85.

## Close calls

**Manfred Kaltz (West Germany, Hamburg).** 581 Bundesliga games for HSV with 76 goals, 53 of them penalties, a Bundesliga record. His curling crosses were known as "Bananenflanken". He won the 1983 European Cup, three league titles and Euro 1980, with 69 caps.

**Gianluca Zambrotta (Italy, Juventus, Barcelona and Milan).** 100 Italy caps, the 2006 World Cup and a place in that tournament's All-Star team, having opened the scoring in the 3-0 quarter-final win over Ukraine. He played on both flanks, which counts against him in a list judged on one.

## How the right-back has changed

The order of this list traces the position's history. Djalma Santos in the 1950s and Carlos Alberto in 1970 showed that a Brazilian full-back could decide World Cups. Vogts and Bergomi were defenders first, picked to stop the best forward on the other side. Cafu combined both jobs over three World Cup finals, Dani Alves pushed the role towards the wing, and Lahm showed it could be played from midfield too.
