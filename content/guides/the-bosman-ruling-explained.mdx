The Bosman ruling was a 1995 European Court of Justice decision that reshaped football's transfer system almost overnight. It came from a single player's legal fight, but its effects are still visible in every transfer window.

## The case behind the name

Jean-Marc Bosman, a Belgian midfielder, wanted to leave RFC Liege for French club Dunkerque once his contract expired in 1990. Liege demanded a fee anyway, Dunkerque refused to pay it, and the move collapsed. Bosman sued, arguing the rule breached EU law on the free movement of workers.

## What the court decided

In December 1995, the European Court of Justice sided with Bosman on two counts:

- Clubs can no longer demand a fee for a player once his contract has expired
- EU competitions can no longer limit how many players from other EU countries a club fields

## Why it changed the transfer market

Before Bosman, even an out-of-contract player could be held to ransom by his old club. After the ruling, players gained the leverage to run down their deals and leave for nothing, forcing clubs to negotiate extensions earlier or risk losing top talent for free. It's the same principle behind every modern "free agent" transfer.

## The knock-on effect on squads

Scrapping the foreign player quota had just as big an impact. Clubs could suddenly build squads without worrying about nationality limits, which is a major reason top European leagues became so dominated by multinational rosters in the decades since.
