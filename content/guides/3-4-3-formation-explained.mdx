**The 3-4-3 is a formation with three centre-backs, a midfield four of two central midfielders flanked by two wing-backs, and a front three, most commonly a centre-forward with two No.10s just behind him.** That version is also written as 3-4-2-1, a label the Premier League's own explainer treats as another name for the same shape.

The Premier League's [tactics explainer on the 3-4-3](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4244194), produced with Coaches' Voice, puts it in one line: "The 3-4-3 is a formation that uses three central defenders and, ahead of them, a double pivot in central midfield." Its appeal is balance. "It commits five outfield players to attacks, while the other five cover underneath and focus more on defending."

## Where it came from

Back threes are old. The Premier League explainer points to the W-M, "first used in the early part of the 20th century", as the first consistent back three, and says the 3-4-3 "is thought to have first been used in Italy, where three central defenders in a 3-5-2 was common", before becoming a more offensive version of that shape. Take one central midfielder out of a [3-5-2](/guides/3-5-2-formation-explained), push a forward up in his place, and you have a 3-4-3.

There is a second route in, from a back four. The explainer notes that "Johan Cruyff set his Barcelona team up in a 4-3-3 shape, but often converted this into a 3-4-3" by stepping a defender into midfield, and that Mauricio Pochettino "often used an attacking 3-4-3 shape converted from a 4-3-3 starting formation" at Southampton and Tottenham. A 3-4-3 can still appear this way: a team lines up with a back four and becomes a back three only once it has the ball.

## The shape, line by line

**Three centre-backs.** The middle one organises; the two either side are the players a 3-4-3 depends on. The explainer says the wider centre-backs can push forward and dribble into midfield, and gives the example of Gareth Southgate's England, with "Kyle Walker playing as the right-sided centre-back".

**Two wing-backs.** They provide all of the team's natural width. In possession they push high, overlap and cross; out of possession they drop level with the centre-backs. Our guide to [full-backs, wing-backs and inverted full-backs](/guides/fullback-vs-wingback-vs-inverted-fullback) covers how the role differs from a full-back in a back four.

**The double pivot.** Two central midfielders link the defence to the front three. This is the line that separates the 3-4-3 from the 3-5-2: there are two players in the middle, not three, so both have to defend and both have to pass. A common split is one who carries the ball and one who sits deeper, as N'Golo Kanté and Jorginho did for Tuchel's Chelsea.

**The front three.** The explainer lists three set-ups: a narrow trio with two No.10s behind a centre-forward, a flat line with two wide forwards, or a false nine with wide players attacking the centre. The centre-forward can be any of the profiles in our guide to [types of striker](/guides/types-of-striker-explained).

## What each player is asked to do

| Position | With the ball | Without the ball |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goalkeeper | Plays short to the back three | Sweeps behind the back line |
| Wide centre-backs | Step out and carry the ball into midfield | Cover the channel when a wing-back is upfield |
| Central centre-back | Organises, switches play | Marks the striker, defends the box |
| Wing-backs | Stay wide and high, overlap, cross | Drop into a back five |
| Double pivot | Links defence to attack, feeds the front three | Screens the back three, covers the middle |
| No.10s or wide forwards | Find space between the lines, create and finish | Screen passes into midfield, press the build-up |
| Centre-forward | Occupies the centre-backs, links play, finishes | Leads the press |

## How it changes without the ball

"A 3-4-3 will become a 5-2-3 or 5-4-1 when the team is out of possession," the Premier League explainer says. The wing-backs retreat to make a back five; what the front three do decides which of the two shapes appears.

In a 5-2-3 the front three stay high, screen passes or press to push the opponent wide. In a 5-4-1 the two No.10s drop alongside the double pivot and the side defends in two banks, with only the striker left upfield.

Tuchel's Chelsea switched between shapes in the 2021 Champions League final. Coaches' Voice's [analysis of the game](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/manchester-city-0-chelsea-1-tactical-analysis/) says they "defended with a 5-2-3 or, if City had a particularly strong grasp of possession, a 5-3-1-1".

## Strengths

**Balance.** Five attacking, five covering, by design. "The 3-4-3 provides good balance between attack and defence," the explainer says.

**Central overloads.** Two narrow No.10s stand in the spaces either side of the opposition's midfield, and with the striker that gives three players between the lines against a back four.

**A secure centre.** The back three and the double pivot form a five-man base in front of goal, which, in the explainer's words, provides "a strong base against counter-attacks through the centre of the pitch".

**Pressing high.** Three forwards can engage a back four's centre-backs and one full-back at once, something the two strikers of a 3-5-2 cannot.

## Weaknesses

**The flanks on the break.** The explainer's first warning: "Opposition counter-attacks can target wide areas, as the back line of three covers less space across the pitch" than a back four. A wing-back caught upfield leaves a wide centre-back defending in open space.

**Two in midfield.** "The central midfield pair, meanwhile, can be overloaded following a defensive transition." A side with three central midfielders, a 4-3-3 for example, starts with a spare player there.

**The wing-backs' legs.** "As the 3-4-3 often uses two No 10s, there is also a significant physical demand on the wing-backs," the explainer notes: they run the whole flank in both directions, which makes them obvious candidates for the changes set out in [football substitution rules](/guides/football-substitution-rules-explained).

**A system that needs specific players.** Without wide centre-backs comfortable on the ball and wing-backs who can both defend and cross, the shape loses its point. That was the charge against it at Manchester United, below.

## The teams that made it famous

| Team | Seasons | What it won |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Antonio Conte's Chelsea | 2016-17 | Premier League with a record 30 wins and 93 points |
| Thomas Tuchel's Chelsea | 2020-21 | Champions League, 1-0 v Manchester City |
| Ruben Amorim's Sporting | 2020-21, 2023-24 | Two Portuguese titles |
| Oliver Glasner's Crystal Palace | 2024-25 | FA Cup, the club's first major trophy |

**Conte's Chelsea.** Conte started his first six Premier League games with a 4-1-4-1. After defeats by Liverpool and Arsenal in September 2016, [the Premier League reported](https://www.premierleague.com/news/117401), he "switched to a 3-4-3" for the trip to Hull, with Marcos Alonso and Victor Moses as wing-backs, and the system "morphed into a 3-4-2-1 when in possession". Chelsea won 2-0. [UEFA records](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/0239-0e96fac6a3e4-cddf5b3d2349-1000--chelsea-secure-premier-league-title-in-conte-s-first-season) that 13 consecutive league wins followed, ended by a 2-0 defeat at Tottenham, and that the title was sealed with a 1-0 win at West Brom on 12 May 2017. The [Premier League's season review](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/693686) says Chelsea "claimed 30 wins, the most by any team in a Premier League season, to rack up 93 points". The explainer describes the shape as "a 3-4-3 with Diego Costa supported by two inside forwards"; Costa was the team's top scorer with 20.

**Tuchel's Chelsea.** In the 2021 Champions League final in Porto, Coaches' Voice describes Chelsea in a "3-4-2-1 formation": César Azpilicueta, Thiago Silva and Antonio Rüdiger at the back, Reece James and Ben Chilwell at wing-back, Jorginho and N'Golo Kanté in midfield, and Timo Werner, Mason Mount and Kai Havertz up front. [UEFA's match report](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/0269-12650cedca55-37515f69768e-1000--report-chelsea-claim-second-title/) has Havertz scoring the only goal three minutes before half-time from Mount's pass, giving Chelsea their second Champions League title, with Kanté named player of the match. The explainer singles out how Tuchel's wing-backs "push forward and rotate with the two No 10s" to reach scoring positions.

**Amorim's Sporting and Manchester United.** At Sporting, Amorim [ended a 19-year wait](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4160039) for the Portuguese title in 2021 and won a second in 2023-24, ten points ahead of Benfica. He was appointed by Manchester United on 1 November 2024 and kept his back three; the Premier League's [2024-25 formation review](https://www.premierleague.com/news/4323030) names him as a 3-4-2-1 devotee. United [finished 15th](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4530967/man-utd-announce-ruben-amorim-departure) in his first season and lost the Europa League final to Tottenham, and he left on 5 January 2026 with the side sixth. [Sky Sports reported](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/13490674/ruben-amorim-sacked-why-manchester-united-felt-they-had-no-choice-but-to-remove-portuguese-coach) that "this was not a sacking based around Amorim's preference for 3-4-3", but around his refusal to adapt tactics as circumstances changed.

**Glasner's Crystal Palace.** The same review names Oliver Glasner as a 3-4-2-1 coach. In May 2025 his Palace side beat Manchester City 1-0 in the FA Cup final, Eberechi Eze scoring in the 16th minute, to win [their first major trophy](https://www.beinsports.com/en-us/soccer/fa-cup/articles/crystal-palace-1-0-manchester-city-eze-seals-clubs-first-major-trophy-in-fa-cup-final-2025-05-17).

## Is the 3-4-3 on the rise?

In its 3-4-2-1 form, yes. The Premier League's 2024-25 review called it "the biggest climber in terms of formations": 71 uses in 2023-24 became 117 in 2024-25, with Glasner, Amorim and Wolves' Vitor Pereira its main users. Over the same period the [4-3-3](/guides/4-3-3-formation-explained) fell from 172 uses to 114, while the [4-2-3-1](/guides/4-2-3-1-formation-explained) rose from 296 to 408 and was the primary shape of 13 of the 20 clubs.

The two have more in common than the numbers suggest. Both play a double pivot with two No.10s and a striker in front of it; the difference is whether the width comes from wing-backs with three centre-backs behind, or from full-backs and wide attackers in front of a back four.

## 3-4-3 against the other common shapes

| Opponent | Where the 3-4-3 has the edge | Where it is exposed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4-4-2 | Three centre-backs against two strikers; No.10s find space between the lines | Wingers and full-backs doubling up on a wing-back |
| 4-3-3 | Three forwards press the back four; wing-backs pin the full-backs | Three central midfielders against the double pivot |
| 4-2-3-1 | Spare centre-back against the lone striker | Wide attackers running into the space behind the wing-backs |
| 3-5-2 | Three forwards against three centre-backs | Outnumbered three against two in central midfield |

## When a coach picks it

A 3-4-3 suits a squad with three good centre-backs, at least two of them comfortable carrying the ball, wing-backs fit enough to cover the flank all game, two midfielders who can both pass and defend, and creative players who want to work between the lines rather than on the touchline. It suits a coach who wants to press with three and still keep five behind the ball.

It suits a squad less when the full-backs are not natural wing-backs or when the opponent overloads central midfield, because those are the two places the shape is thinnest. Its closest relatives are covered in our guides to the [3-5-2](/guides/3-5-2-formation-explained) and the [4-4-2](/guides/4-4-2-formation-explained).
