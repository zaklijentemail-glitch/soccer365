**A full-back defends on the flank of a back four, a wing-back patrols the whole flank in front of a back three, and an inverted full-back defends like a full-back but moves into central midfield when his team has the ball.** All three start in the same wide area of the pitch. What separates them is the shape around them and where they go once the ball is won.

The same player can move between them. Trent Alexander-Arnold broke into Liverpool's side as an overlapping right-back and later became one of the best-known inverted full-backs in the Premier League. To see why that matters, it helps to take each role in turn.

## The three roles at a glance

| | Full-back | Wing-back | Inverted full-back |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Back line without the ball | Back four | Back five | Back four |
| Typical formations | 4-4-2, 4-3-3, 4-2-3-1 | 3-5-2, 3-4-3, 3-4-1-2 | 4-3-3, 4-2-3-1 |
| Where he goes with the ball | Up the touchline, outside or inside the winger | High and wide, often in the front line | Inside, into central midfield |
| Who provides width | Shared with the winger | Almost always the wing-back | The winger |
| Main physical demand | Repeated sprints up and down one flank | Covering the entire flank for 90 minutes | Positional discipline and quick recovery |
| Key technical skill | Crossing, one-against-one defending | Crossing from deep and the byline | Passing and receiving under pressure |
| Defining examples | Nílton and Djalma Santos, Robertson, Alba | Moses and Alonso at Chelsea, 2016-17 | Lahm at Bayern, Cancelo, Zinchenko, Alexander-Arnold |

## The full-back

The name comes from the 2-3-5 of the early 20th century, in which the full-backs were the deepest players on the pitch. As [Coaches' Voice](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/modern-full-back-explained-alexander-arnold-robertson-alba/) explains, once the back four took over, the full-backs moved out to the sides. Brazil's 1958 World Cup winners were an early exception to the defensive stereotype, with Nílton Santos and Djalma Santos both willing to attack.

<Formation shape="4-3-3" roles="GK,LB,CB,CB,RB,CM,DM,CM,LW,ST,RW" lift="LB:0.55,RB:0.55" caption="Full-backs in a 4-3-3: pushed up beside the midfield to overlap or underlap the wingers" />

**Without the ball** a full-back defends the space outside his centre-back, deals with the opposing winger one-against-one, cuts off balls down the line and chases back when possession is lost. In a pressing side he also jumps out to close down the opposing full-back.

**With the ball** the modern full-back's job is to join attacks with two kinds of run. An overlap goes round the outside of the winger in front of him; an underlap goes up the inside. Either one gives the team a two-against-one against the opposing full-back, the most common overload in wide areas. From there he crosses, cuts the ball back or switches play. When the winger in front comes inside, the full-back becomes the team's width.

Liverpool's full-backs under Jürgen Klopp set the modern benchmark for attacking output. Alexander-Arnold set the record for assists by a defender in a single Premier League season with 12 in 2018-19, then [broke it with 13](https://www.liverpoolfc.com/news/first-team/403093-trent-alexander-arnold-breaks-his-own-premier-league-assist-record) in 2019-20, the 13th coming in a 5-3 win over Chelsea. On the other side, Andy Robertson was the Premier League's all-time leader for assists by a defender until Alexander-Arnold passed him in a [3-1 win over Burnley in February 2024](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3889135).

By May 2025, according to the [Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4303483), Alexander-Arnold had 64 and Robertson 59.

## The wing-back

A wing-back is the wide player in a formation with three centre-backs. [Coaches' Voice](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/wing-backs-football-tactics-explained-conte-tuchel/) describes the role as a hybrid of defender and midfielder, and the word itself as a blend of full-back and winger. It traces the back five to Helenio Herrera's Inter in the 1960s and the more attacking version of the role to Carlos Bilardo's Argentina in the 1980s.

<Formation shape="3-4-3" roles="GK,LCB,CB,RCB,LWB,CM,CM,RWB,LW,ST,RW" lift="LWB:0.45,RWB:0.45" caption="Wing-backs in a 3-4-3: the only players on the touchlines, pushed high in possession" />

The important difference from a full-back is the third centre-back behind him. That extra cover lets a wing-back play higher and wider, and it means he almost always supplies his team's width, because nobody else in the side stands on the touchline. Full-backs only sometimes do.

**With the ball** wing-backs stretch the pitch, cross from deep or from near the byline, and in some systems attack in a front line of five. Increasingly they also take part in wide rotations, moving infield while a wide centre-back or midfielder goes outside them.

**Without the ball** they drop either side of the centre-backs to make a back five and block crosses. Because three centre-backs are covering the middle, a wing-back can leave the line to engage the ball more freely than a full-back can. In a high press, he may push right up to the opposing full-back.

The cost is distance. A wing-back covers the whole flank, so when the ball is lost the run back is long, and the space he leaves is where opponents counter-attack. That is the weakness our [3-5-2 guide](/guides/3-5-2-formation-explained) describes from the other side.

The most famous recent example came at Chelsea in 2016-17. After a 3-0 defeat at Arsenal in September 2016, Antonio Conte switched to a back three, with [Victor Moses and Marcos Alonso as wing-backs](https://www.skysports.com/football/news/11668/10751410/was-chelsea-s-defeat-to-arsenal-the-day-they-won-the-premier-league). Moses had been a winger. Chelsea then won 13 league games in a row and [clinched the title at West Brom on 12 May 2017](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/0239-0e96fac6a3e4-cddf5b3d2349-1000--chelsea-secure-premier-league-title-in-conte-s-first-season). Converting wingers is a common route into the role: Coaches' Voice lists Ashley Young, Juan Cuadrado, Jesús Navas and Ivan Perišić alongside Moses.

## The inverted full-back

An inverted full-back starts as a normal full-back in a back four. [The Premier League](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3786211) defines the role simply: full-backs who move inside into central spaces when their team has the ball. Without the ball, he defends in the usual wide position.

<Formation shape="3-2-2-3" roles="GK,LB,CB,CB,DM,RB,CM,CM,LW,ST,RW" caption="An inverted right-back in possession: the left-back tucks in to make a back three, the right-back steps up beside the holding midfielder" />

The point is numbers in the middle. When one or both full-backs come inside during build-up, the team gains an extra central midfielder, the midfielders in front can push higher, and the attackers can follow them. The winger keeps the width, and if the opposing winger follows the full-back inside, the winger is left one-against-one with a defender.

Guardiola made this a regular part of build-up play at Bayern Munich with Philipp Lahm and David Alaba, and took it to Manchester City with players such as João Cancelo and Kyle Walker. He was not the first coach to do it, though. [Coaches' Voice](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/inverted-full-backs-guardiola-cancelo-trent-alexander-arnold-lahm-football-tactics/) notes that Johan Cruyff's Barcelona would sometimes turn their 4-3-3 into a 3-4-3 diamond by moving one full-back into midfield, with Guardiola playing as the single pivot.

By November 2023 the Premier League reported that Mikel Arteta, Jürgen Klopp, Ange Postecoglou, Roberto De Zerbi and, at times, Erik ten Hag were all inverting their full-backs. At Arsenal, Oleksandr Zinchenko was the player who stepped into midfield, and [Myles Lewis-Skelly took over the role in 2024-25](https://learning.coachesvoice.com/cv/3-2-4-1-formation-tactics-explained/).

### How Liverpool used it

Alexander-Arnold's move came against league leaders Arsenal at Anfield on 9 April 2023. Liverpool were 2-0 down and drew 2-2, with Alexander-Arnold stepping into midfield beside Fabinho in possession to make a 3-2-2-3. "In the build-up, Trent played more inside. Double six, that's how it is," [Klopp said afterwards](https://www.90min.com/posts/why-jurgen-klopp-changed-trent-alexander-arnold-role-against-arsenal).

Liverpool won their next seven games, and according to [Goal](https://www.goal.com/en/lists/trent-alexander-arnold-best-position-liverpool-midfield-right-back/bltfe442f170cb7b43d) Alexander-Arnold had seven assists in the last ten league games of the season after two before the switch. He said of it: "I felt I could dictate and control from right-back, but this was another dimension to my game."

### Inverted full-back or stepping centre-back?

Guardiola has since got the same midfield numbers a different way. Instead of a full-back coming inside, a centre-back steps up beside the holding midfielder: John Stones, Fernandinho and Manuel Akanji have all played alongside Rodri in the build-up at City. The result looks similar on the pitch, a back three behind a double pivot, but the player stepping out is a defender used to central areas, not one used to the touchline.

## Strengths and weaknesses compared

| Role | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full-back | Balanced; creates wide two-against-ones with the winger; four defenders behind the ball when the full-backs hold position | When both push on, the space behind them is open to switches of play |
| Wing-back | Width on both flanks without wingers; five defenders without the ball; freedom to jump out of the line | Long recovery runs; gaps left out wide; very high physical load; fewer players in midfield and attack |
| Inverted full-back | Extra central midfielder in build-up; better counter-pressing in the middle; isolates the winger one-against-one | One fewer player in the last line on transitions; needs a rare player who can defend wide and pass like a midfielder |

The Coaches' Voice summary of the inverted role's main drawback is blunt: it needs players who can defend in wide areas but also handle the ball like a central midfielder, and when the ball is lost there is one fewer player in the last line of defence.

## Why a coach chooses one over another

**Full-backs** suit a back four with wingers who stay wide or come inside, the shape of a [4-2-3-1](/guides/4-2-3-1-formation-explained) or a [4-4-2](/guides/4-4-2-formation-explained). The double pivot in a 4-2-3-1 in particular is what lets both full-backs attack at once.

**Wing-backs** suit a squad with three good centre-backs, a shortage of wingers and two wide players fit enough to run the flank all game. They also suit a side that expects to spend long spells without the ball, because the back five forms without any change of personnel.

**Inverted full-backs** suit a possession side that wants to control the middle during build-up and has wingers good enough to win one-against-ones.

The roles are not as separate as the labels suggest. A wing-back can drift inside on a rotation, an inverted full-back still defends wide, and in a 3-4-3 a wide centre-back may overlap like a full-back. Formation data from 2023-24 shows how much the back four still dominates, though: after 12 rounds, [80.8 per cent of Premier League line-ups](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/3786211) used one, and back-three line-ups were at their lowest share since 2016-17. That makes the full-back, overlapping or inverted, still the most common wide defender in the league.
