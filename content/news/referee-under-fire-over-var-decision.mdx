A former Premier League referee has strongly criticised the VAR officials at last night's match, arguing that a clear penalty was missed despite the tournament's extensive camera coverage.

## What went wrong

According to the former official, not one of the tournament's 48 pitch-side cameras captured a clean angle of the incident, leaving the VAR team unable to meet the "clear and obvious error" threshold needed to overturn the referee's original call. Without that certainty, the decision on the pitch stood, even though replays from other angles suggested a foul may have occurred.

## Why it has caused a stir

The incident has reignited debate over the consistency of VAR decisions, with pundits divided on whether the call should have been overturned regardless of camera coverage. Critics argue that a tournament with dozens of cameras should never be left without a usable angle on a major penalty shout, while others point out that VAR is only ever as reliable as the footage available to it.

## The bigger picture

This isn't the first time camera coverage, rather than the review process itself, has been blamed for a contentious VAR outcome. It's likely to renew calls for organisers to review camera positioning around the penalty area ahead of future knockout matches.
