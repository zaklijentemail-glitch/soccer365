Pubs and bars in England and Wales will be allowed to stay open until 30 minutes after the final whistle of England's World Cup quarter-final against Norway, the government has confirmed. The extension covers the whole match, even if kick-off is delayed by extreme heat.

The Home Office announced the move on Saturday, saying the same rules would apply to any further England games, including a potential final. Venues will not need to apply individually, as the extension applies across the board.

## Why the extension was needed

England kick off against Norway at 22:00 BST (18:00 local time) at the Hard Rock Stadium in Miami. The decision follows confusion before England's last-16 match against Mexico on 2 July, when the game was delayed by an hour due to storms and reports of lightning near the stadium.

Policing Minister Sarah Jones said the extension would give fans certainty after that experience.

> "England fans deserve the chance to watch every minute of the quarter-final together, and that is exactly what our extension guarantees. After the weather delay we saw before the Mexico game, we want to give fans and venues complete certainty that no-one will miss a moment of the action."
>
> - Sarah Jones, Policing Minister

Jones added that she hoped pubs would be "packed" as England try to move one step closer to "bringing football home."

## How the hours have changed

Licensing hours had already been extended for the tournament:

- Games kicking off between 17:00 and 21:00: venues can stay open until 01:00 (extended from 23:00).
- Games kicking off between 21:00 and 22:00: venues can stay open until 02:00.
- For the Norway match specifically: venues can stay open until 30 minutes after the final whistle, regardless of delays.

The home secretary holds the legal power to extend licensing hours for events of "exceptional international, national or local significance."

## Heat and weather concerns in Miami

High temperatures have been a recurring issue throughout the World Cup, co-hosted by Mexico, Canada and the United States. FIFA has introduced mandatory hydration breaks in each half to help players cope.

> "It's going to be a tough day for players and spectators alike in Miami, as heat and humidity levels soar."
>
> - Matt Taylor, BBC lead weather presenter

The National Weather Service has issued a heat advisory for Miami between 11:00 and 19:00 local time, with temperatures peaking at 33C (91.4F) but feeling closer to 43C with humidity.

- Risk of storms at kick-off time: 20-30%.
- Any lightning within eight miles of the stadium would trigger an automatic 30-minute delay.
- FIFA uses the Wet Bulb Globe Temperature (WBGT) index to assess player safety.

Players' union FIFPRO recommends delaying or postponing matches when the WBGT exceeds 28C. The current forecast puts Miami's wet bulb temperature at 27C, and there is no indication the match will be postponed.
