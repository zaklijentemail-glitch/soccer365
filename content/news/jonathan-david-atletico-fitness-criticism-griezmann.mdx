Jonathan David has not yet kicked a ball in a competitive match for Atletico Madrid. He has completed a handful of training sessions under Diego Simeone. On that evidence, a section of the fanbase has reached a verdict, and the verdict is about his body.

Posts and comment threads have gone up questioning the Canadian's physical condition since the first training images circulated. The word being used is overweight.

## Griezmann answered from Florida

The pushback came from an unusual direction. Antoine Griezmann, who left for Orlando in MLS but is still one of the most popular figures the club has produced this century, went into the comments under an Instagram video attacking David and replied directly.

> "Maybe you're the one who is overweight, little monster."
>
> - Antoine Griezmann

It is a throwaway line. It is also the sort of thing former players almost never do, because the safe move is silence. Griezmann knows exactly how the Metropolitano treats a striker who arrives with a question mark attached, having been that striker himself on his return from Barcelona.

## Our view: this is the worst possible moment to judge him

A few things are worth holding onto before anyone decides what David is.

He is a forward who has changed country, league and pre-season cycle mid-stream. Players arriving on [a loan with an option to buy](/guides/what-does-on-loan-mean-football) frequently turn up carrying the physical residue of a summer spent in limbo, training away from a group that was not planning around them. That is not a character flaw. It is what happens when a transfer takes until the end of the window to resolve.

There is also the matter of what a training-ground photograph actually proves. Compression kit, a bad angle and a still frame have convicted a lot of professional athletes who were, in fact, fine. The honest position on 4 September is that nobody watching from outside knows what his body fat reading is, and the people who do know have said nothing.

The last part is the one that matters most. Simeone's teams are among the most physically demanding in Europe to play in. If David genuinely arrived short of condition, that will surface in minutes played long before it surfaces in a comment section, and it will be settled by October rather than debated.

## The loan structure makes this more interesting than it looks

Underneath the noise sits a genuinely unusual arrangement. Juventus are watching, closely, because in June the two clubs are expected to reopen talks over a permanent move, and the price of that conversation is set by what David does in La Liga, the Champions League and the Copa del Rey.

That produces a strange alignment. Atletico want him to succeed because they are the ones playing him. Juventus want him to succeed because a productive season raises what they can ask for, or improves whatever they get back if the option is exercised. Everybody in the deal is rooting for the same outcome, from opposite sides of the table.

The people not rooting for it, right now, are a portion of his own new support.

## Key facts at a glance

- David joined Atletico Madrid on loan from Juventus with an option to buy.
- He has completed his first training sessions with Simeone's squad; his competitive debut is still to come.
- Sections of the fanbase have criticised his physical condition on social media.
- Griezmann, now at Orlando, replied to one of the attacking posts in David's defence.
- Juventus and Atletico are set to reopen transfer talks in June, based on his performances across all three competitions.
- David was born in 2000 and is a Canada international.
