Norway have reached the World Cup quarter-finals for the first time in the country's history, beating five-time champions Brazil 2-1 in New Jersey thanks to a second-half brace from Erling Haaland.

## How the match unfolded

Brazil dominated large spells of possession and created the better early chances, but could not find a way past a well-organised Norwegian defence sitting deep and compact. The breakthrough came against the run of play midway through the second half, when Haaland converted from close range after a quick counter-attack. He doubled Norway's lead eleven minutes later with a clinical finish, leaving Brazil to chase the game from behind for the closing stages.

## Why this result matters

The win is a historic moment for Norwegian football. Despite producing a string of elite individual talents in recent years, the national team had never previously gone beyond the last 16 at a World Cup. Beating a five-time champion to do it only adds to the significance.

For Brazil, the exit ends the tournament earlier than expected and will renew scrutiny on the head coach's tactical approach, after a talented squad was unable to break down a deep defensive setup when it mattered most.

## What happens next

The result sets up a quarter-final meeting for Norway against the winner of the other second-round tie in their half of the draw. Brazil, meanwhile, head home from the tournament at the last-16 stage.
