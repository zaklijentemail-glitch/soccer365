Edin Dzeko has confirmed he will stop playing for Bosnia and Herzegovina after the Nations League match against Sweden on 2 October. He is 40 years old, still contracted at Schalke, and still his country's first-choice centre-forward. That combination is the whole story.

The numbers he leaves behind are worth sitting with for a second: 151 caps, 73 goals. He is Bosnia's most-capped player and their record scorer at the same time.

That double is rarer than it sounds. Appearance records usually belong to goalkeepers and defenders who play for 15 years without needing to justify their place with output; scoring records belong to forwards who burn brightly for eight or nine. Dzeko did both jobs, in the same career, for a country that has existed as an independent football nation for barely three decades.

## What he said

The announcement came on Instagram, and the framing was about the shirt rather than the statistics.

> "Nothing in my career has ever compared to the pride I have felt every single time I wore that shirt. It was my childhood dream, a dream I carried with me on each of the 151 occasions I had the honour of representing my country on a football pitch. So many things have changed, and I have changed too, but my love for this shirt never has."
>
> - Edin Dzeko

He also offered the clearest possible answer to anyone wondering why a player still good enough to start would walk away.

> "Now I feel the time has come to step aside. If I have made it to the age of 40 with the national team, it is because I truly gave everything I had. Every time. Everything."
>
> - Edin Dzeko

And an invitation.

> "I just hope you will be there that night against Sweden, so we can share one more emotion. That is how I want to experience my final night representing my country: with all of you by my side."
>
> - Edin Dzeko

## The two nights that built the legend

Dzeko debuted against Turkey in 2007, a 21-year-old who had not yet moved to Wolfsburg, let alone Manchester City or Inter. Bosnia at that point had never qualified for anything.

The campaign that changed it was 2014, and he singles out two moments from it.

> "The emotions I felt on the night of the goal against Wales, and then in Zenica against Italy in March, will stay in my heart forever. Seeing my people celebrating in the streets of our cities was another incredible gift that football has given me."
>
> - Edin Dzeko

That is the part outsiders tend to miss about his standing at home. Bosnia's first World Cup was not a routine box ticked by a mid-sized European nation. It was the first time the country appeared at a major tournament as an independent state, and Dzeko's goals were the mechanism.

## He did not fade out

The easy version of a farewell like this involves a veteran being carried by reputation for a couple of years. That is not what happened. Dzeko was central to Bosnia's run to [the last 32 of the 2026 World Cup](/guides/world-cup-2026-format-explained), the expanded tournament's new knockout round, at an age when most forwards have been retired for half a decade.

It also means his exit is not a formality for whoever picks the team next. Bosnia are not replacing a ceremonial captain. They are replacing their starting striker, their reference point in the box, and the man who has scored roughly one international goal every other game for 19 years.

## A generational handover

This is the second time in five weeks that a country has had to plan for life after a player who defined it. Lionel Messi's Argentina retirement came with 207 caps and a World Cup behind it. Dzeko's case is smaller in scale and, in a way, heavier in proportion: Argentina have a production line, Bosnia have a population of about three million.

He addressed that directly, saying there are young players coming through who deserve their spot. It is a generous line. It is also an accurate description of a problem.

## Key facts at a glance

- Dzeko retires from international football after Bosnia's Nations League fixture with Sweden on 2 October.
- He leaves as record scorer (73 goals) and most-capped player (151) for his country.
- Debut came against Turkey in 2007; former clubs include Wolfsburg, Manchester City and Inter.
- He was central to qualification for the 2014 World Cup, Bosnia's first major tournament as an independent nation.
- He played a leading role in the run to the last 32 at the 2026 World Cup.
- He continues in club football with Schalke.
