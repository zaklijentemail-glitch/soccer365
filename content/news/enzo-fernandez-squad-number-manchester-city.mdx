**Enzo Fernández will wear the No.17 shirt at Manchester City.** The Argentina midfielder signed a five-year contract on Tuesday after leaving Chelsea, and the number he has been handed is the interesting part of the announcement.

Seventeen was Kevin De Bruyne's. For ten years, at a club that won more or less everything during them, it belonged to one player so completely that most supporters had stopped thinking of it as a number at all. City confirmed the squad number alongside the signing in their [club announcement](https://www.mancity.com/news/mens).

## Giving away a number nobody expected to see again

Clubs have two options with a shirt like that. They can leave it in a drawer for a few seasons and let the association fade on its own, which is the safe route and the one most take. Or they can put it straight back on the pitch and accept every comparison that follows.

City have chosen the second, and it is not the sort of thing that happens by accident. Squad numbers get discussed. Somebody in that building decided the shirt was better used than protected, and that this was the player to use it.

Fernández, for what it is worth, does not play anything like De Bruyne. There is no attempt here to replace one with the other. What the number does is set a level, and it makes the first difficult month at a new club louder than it would otherwise be.

Before De Bruyne, 17 had a quieter history. Sun Jihai wore it, the first Chinese international to make a real career in English football. So did Martin Petrov, the Bulgarian winger from the years when City were still assembling rather than dominating.

## A 25-year-old who has already won the big ones

There is a strange shape to Fernández's career so far. Most midfielders spend their twenties building towards the tournaments that define them. He has already had his.

Argentina won the World Cup in 2022 with him in the side, and the Copa América followed in 2024. Both arrived before his club career in Europe had properly settled. He came through at Defensa y Justicia, moved to River Plate, then to Benfica, and spent his most recent seasons at Chelsea.

So City are signing someone who has nothing left to prove internationally and, at 25, has not yet reached the years when a midfielder is usually at his best. That combination is rarer than it sounds, and it is worth more than any single performance you could point to.

## The Chelsea end of it

The direction of this deal matters too. Chelsea have let a World Cup winner in his prime years walk to a direct rival, which is not a decision any club makes comfortably or explains fully in a press release.

Fernández arrives as part of a broader reshaping under Enzo Maresca. Elliot Anderson, Gerónimo Rulli and Ayyoub Bouaddi had already joined before him, and Iliman Ndiaye came through the door as the window shut on [deadline day](/guides/how-transfer-windows-work). Five signings is not tinkering. It is a squad being rebuilt through the middle while the season is already moving.

## Key facts at a glance

- Fernández wears the **No.17** at Manchester City.
- He joined from Chelsea on Tuesday on a five-year contract.
- Kevin De Bruyne wore the number for a decade; Sun Jihai and Martin Petrov had it before him.
- He is 25, and has won the World Cup (2022) and Copa América (2024) with Argentina.
- Previous clubs: Defensa y Justicia, River Plate, Benfica, Chelsea.
- City's other summer arrivals: Elliot Anderson, Gerónimo Rulli, Ayyoub Bouaddi and Iliman Ndiaye.
